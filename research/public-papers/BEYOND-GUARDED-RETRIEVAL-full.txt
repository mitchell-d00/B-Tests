Abstract 
Multi-Platform Linked Public Building (MPLPB), developed by Mitchell D. McPhetridge, implements a local 
source ledger with integrity checks, provenance, guarded revisions, and bounded source-supported answers. 
We evaluate an identified Combined MPLPB release across six engineering experiments involving native 
regression tests, structured relation transfer, synthetic scaling, source retrieval, and historical communication 
reconstruction. We also examine the development evidence of Object-Oriented Cognition and Continuity 
(OOCC), whose V3 crawler succeeded the historical V2 architecture, to establish a comparable standard for 
version-specific technical claims. 
All 58 selected upstream MPLPB regression tests passed. On a 515-page text projection, the expected source 
appeared among the first five results for 31 of 49 exact-phrase questions; richly specified 
communication-header queries placed it first in 40 of 42 cases. Seventy-two queries over 3,962 prestructured 
relation rows distinguished direct status retrieval from multi-relation requests. The deterministic reader abstained 
on 57 experimental event-reconstruction question forms that fell outside its finite grammar. Across these 
experiments, the implementation preserved important source-safety properties but did not demonstrate general 
reconstruction of event identities, cross-source relationships, and partial corrections through its strict-answer 
interface. 
We propose separately labeled retrieval diagnostics, source-occurrence and event-candidate identities, 
versioned temporal relationships, and optional evidence-pinned relation composition. The proposed changes 
preserve MPLPB’s authority controls while creating testable routes from source location to historical 
reconstruction. A common, dated evidentiary standard for competing architectures is essential: earlier criticism 
must be examined against the implementation that existed when it was made, and measured behavior must 
take precedence over presentation order or technical rhetoric. 
Keywords: 
MPLPB; OOCC; provenance; source-backed question answering; temporal reconstruction; iterative engineering; experiment 
provenance; anchoring; evaluation bias; abstention. 
1. Introduction 
Evidence-preserving systems must perform distinct operations: retain sources, establish their identity, determine 
present answer eligibility, retrieve relevant passages, and reconstruct qualified historical claims. Successfully 
locating a passage does not necessarily recover its speaker, event identity, correction history, or connection to 
other records. Conversely, a previous source may lose authority for present answers while remaining important 
evidence of an earlier claim. The latter distinction—
current authority versus historical relevance
—is central 
to this study. 
Combined MPLPB provides an unusually explicit separation between protected source records, eligibility rules, 
and supported answers [M1–M5]. Its bounded deterministic reasoning is a deliberate safeguard against

2. Chronology and comparative evidence 
A sound technical comparison reconstructs each system’s actual state at the time of a test. OOCC developed 
through repeated operational trials and revisions rather than as a single static specification. Its superseded V2 
crawler contributed archived graph recovery, checkpoint verification, and failure records; 
V3 is the successor 
crawler architecture 
and has its own source-scoped A/B lanes, working-state records, and experiments [O3,

successor at its own recorded checkpoints. 
The 2026 correspondence between Gelhaus and McPhetridge contains technical criticisms and reports of 
earlier OOCC field diagnostics [H1, H2]. Those claims are assessed as dated evidence. The study applies the 
same standard to the published MPLPB implementation: direct execution, observed results, retained source 
identity, and stated scope are evaluated separately from broader conceptual interpretation. 
2.1 Early critique, field tests, and implementation chronology 
McPhetridge’s correspondence identifies four failure modes worth testing: human audit limits, silently omitted 
records, invalid source claims gaining authority, and loss of explanatory history through compression [H1]. 
These are specific failure hypotheses rather than findings about every later OOCC revision. Gelhaus’s 
contemporaneous correspondence reports field experiments involving continuity letters, structured versus 
unstructured external state, different AI platforms, and restructuring under an unexpected change in the 
governing problem [H1]. Those observations establish a history of attempted interventions, observable 
difficulties, and subsequent changes, although the field conditions differed from a matched laboratory design. 
A valid finding about a historical version requires its dated claim, contemporaneous source and code state, 
direct test evidence, and any fix already present when the criticism was made. A later working mechanism 
cannot automatically establish that an earlier version contained the same fix; an early critique likewise cannot 
describe a changed version without renewed testing. Gelhaus’s later correspondence distinguishes ideas 
encountered during discussion—including crawlers and SQL—from the timing and authorship of specific later 
implementations [H2]. Source-to-code chronology, rather than either participant’s retrospective characterization, 
resolves implementation history.

2.3 A common standard of evidence 
For both systems, the evaluation sequence is 
dated claim → identifiable source → executable test or 
observed field event → integrity/readback record → scoped outcome
. Field observations, inspected source 
code, native tests, and later independent replication occupy different evidentiary levels, regardless of which

evidence, rather than inheriting authority from their date, vocabulary, or position in the reading order. 
3. What MPLPB actually implements 
3.1 Local integrity, eligibility and guarded answers 
The Combined MPLPB ledger uses source IDs, content hashes, origin/depth checks, lineage references and 
source eligibility rules. Its local strict reader relies on a finite grammar and source-body support. The 
deterministic proof graph is intentionally bounded: it does not authorize unconstrained causal reversal, 
cross-page completion, synonym guessing or multi-hop inference [M1]. That policy prevents some plausible but 
unjustified responses. A safe abstention can be a correct result. 
The general answer pipeline makes a further distinction between deterministic support and explicitly configured 
model-assisted assessment. In the latter mode, cited model output remains separately labeled and does not

3.2 Retained versions and historical revision 
MPLPB’s supersession policy retains withdrawn and replaced pages and requires a valid successor before 
retiring its predecessor. It does not automatically reactivate an old source when its replacement is withdrawn. A 
stale derivative remains withheld until rechecked [M3]. Thus, MPLPB’s preservation of historical bytes and its 
current-answer eligibility are established safeguards. 
Historical retrieval, cross-document event identity, and

remain additional functions to evaluate. 
3.3 Specialist work continuity 
The implemented local specialist coordinator, 
mplpb_work
, describes transactional work history, task state, 
capability fencing, revision gates, challenges, evidence references and handoffs. It explicitly separates a 
source-supported claim from authorization to act [M4]. This is substantive continuity engineering. The 
documentation places AI providers and operating-system sandboxes outside the local coordinator, so its tested 
work contracts and broader end-to-end execution are distinct components. More importantly for this paper, a 
typed work-history schema is not itself evidence that the strict question reader can reconstruct historical 
communication events from unprepared source texts. 
3.4 Published implementation boundaries 
MPLPB’s production-readiness notes describe a local research system with bounded source-language rules and 
one-trusted-operator assumptions. They explicitly reserve independent answer-quality evaluation, unrestricted 
language competence and shared-service deployment for further work [M5]. The historical pottery results and 
authored synthetic user tests are useful development indicators, not held-out evidence of broad semantic 
accuracy [M1, M2]. This evaluation accepts those declared boundaries rather than redefining MPLPB’s intended 
strict behavior after observing its output.

5. Study design and consolidated results 
5.1 Study populations, outcomes, and independence 
The experiments are 
engineering probes
, not a randomized sample of real user requests. The unit of analysis 
varies: a sealed page, an indexed passage window, a source-location question, a strict reader answer, a 
repeated header signature, or a synthetic relation statement. Reported denominators apply only within the 
named task. The seven source PDFs are a selected, privacy-restricted domain corpus; the 3,962-edge graph is 
a 
structured transfer input
, not a model-extracted gold standard. The source-location targets were selected and 
checked mechanically by the evaluator from the documents and were frozen before each measured run, 
but 
not blinded to the person constructing them
. These labels measure source location under specified wording, 
not correctness of legal, medical, or historical interpretations. 
A strict refusal is assessed under two distinct rubrics: (i) 
safety/faithfulness
—whether it avoids unsupported 
affirmative claims; and (ii) 
capability coverage
—whether the user-requested relationship is returned. A refusal 
can score positively under (i) and negatively under (ii), without implying faulty reasoning inside a grammar that 
never promised to accept the question. Likewise, the passage ranker supplies candidates; it does not assert 
their truth. No global accuracy percentage or statistical generalization is computed by pooling the studies. The 
same non-pooling rule also applies to OOCC’s field observations, 
legacy V2 predecessor 
structural controls, 
successor V3 
retrospective ablations and HLC source-to-code checks. V2 and V3 are a version lineage, not 
contemporaneous independently deployed systems. Counting different experiments is not a matched 
comparison, but failing to count them at all is an evaluation error. The chronology of a criticism is independently 
relevant to whether it described the implementation that actually existed then.

6. Detailed findings 
6.1 A larger graph can be stored without being semantically reconstructed 
The anonymized transfer of the verified 3,962-edge ledger produced 44 sealed native pages, with zero 
structural validator findings. The reader correctly rejected all 18 nonexistent-record controls and answered 14 of 
18 direct-status questions. On the selected relationship questions, no one-edge or two-hop answer was 
produced (0/18 in each category). The restrained interpretation is important: these were evaluator-chosen 
question forms, and MPLPB’s source-graph rules do not promise arbitrary graph traversal. Abstention is 
consistent with design. The result nevertheless demonstrates that 
successful storage of relation statements 
is not the same as a native, general relation-to-answer interface
. It supplies a concrete product decision: 
either continue to define such questions as outside strict scope, or add a distinctly labeled exploratory operation. 
Both choices can be coherent [E1]. 
The original 15-page fixture produced the same architectural signal on a narrower, curated task: one basic 
supported question passed, while ten compound questions abstained. That pilot should be identified as a 
small 
authored requirement-transfer probe
, not as a “4,113-edge run” [O1, E0]. 
6.2 Candidate-set breadth and strict refusals are separate facts 
In a fictional scaling exercise, a native corpus grew from 80 to 5,120 relation statements across 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32 
and 64 pages. At every size, its validator passed; the direct status question returned the expected answer and 
the absent-record question safely returned 
not_in_corpus
. A two-hop question withheld the unsupported 
composition. However, the provenance/source bundle included every page at each corpus size—even for the 
nonexistent identifier. At 64 pages the median local response times across three repetitions were 553.8 ms for 
an existing status, 679.8 ms for an absent record and 369.6 ms for the two-hop request [E2]. 
Crucial clarification: 
The gate’s complete eligible-source list is not necessarily the final ranked set. Broad 
eligibility may be an intentional completeness feature. The engineering concern is whether downstream steps 
repeatedly parse or carry large source sets when exact identifiers are available, and whether a caller can 
distinguish “eligible corpus” from “retrieved evidence supporting this particular question.” The observed timings 
are local workload measurements, not an asymptotic complexity guarantee or a production throughput claim.

rank-one frequency from 7/10 to 4/10; an annotation occupied the first rank in 5/10 original questions [E3]. This 
is not demonstrated answer contamination; it is a

6.5 The value of precise diagnostic outcomes 
When a question falls outside the strict finite grammar, a refusal may be entirely appropriate. But a developer or 
analyst needs to distinguish at least: unsupported question form, no eligible sources, candidate sources found 
without a supported interpretation, source conflict, insufficient fields, and genuine absence after a specified 
bounded search. A generic negative answer collapses distinct intervention opportunities. Existing pipeline 
contract states already have much of the vocabulary to support this distinction [M2]. The recommendation is to 
make those distinctions more visible and machine-checkable, not to turn refusals into optimistic answers.

8. A proposed minimal architecture

1. 
Immutable source store 
— preserves original bytes or authorized source projection, exact identity,

2. 
Eligibility gate

3. 
Evidence locator 
— performs exact field lookup, quote matching and broader passage ranking,

Historical relation workspace (optional)

authority.

Answer/review gate

9. How to test the proposed improvements fairly 
9.1 Pre-specify task types, not a preferred winner 
An independent reviewer should freeze a corpus and score rubric before either implementation sees the labeled 
evaluation questions. Include distinct categories: direct quotation location; multiple occurrences of one 
communication; different communications with the same subject; identical headers with changed bodies; 
speaker versus quoted speaker; earliest-observed revisions; partial retractions; genuine and apparent 
contradictions; multi-hop paths with and without a missing bridge; conflicting authority; dormant association 
brought into relevance by a later event; and decisions that must abstain. Include both valid positives and 
near-miss negatives. The earlier 15-page authored fixture and 42 header queries are engineering baselines, not 
substitutes for this independent test. 
9.2 Separate different kinds of error 
Report retrieval 
Recall@k
, exact-source attribution, relationship precision/recall, contradiction qualification, false 
entity merges, unsupported affirmative rate, correct abstention, false abstention on supported examples, 
correction-scope accuracy, lineage fidelity, runtime cost and reviewer labor. Aggregate only within comparable 
test groups. A 
not_in_corpus 
result on unsupported grammar should not be counted as a demonstrated 
hallucination or as proof of failed semantic reasoning; it is a 
coverage/contract decision
. Conversely, 
identifying the right source page does not certify the correctness of an inferred relationship. 
9.3 Preserve the intended system boundary 
Run (A) stock MPLPB strict; (B) stock MPLPB assisted with a pinned, independently available local model if 
authorized and feasible; (C) the optional event/relationship extension; and (D) a simple structured index or 
standard retrieval baseline. If a direct OOCC comparison is desired, use the same 
raw original sources 
and 
matched budgets rather than giving one system the other’s precomputed graph. Test the actual connected tools 
and platforms separately from algorithms: an accessible PostgreSQL server, a populated Supabase index or a 
GitHub runner cannot stand in as evidence of semantic accuracy. The local PostgreSQL Messenger route and a 
connected secondary Supabase index were not part of the MPLPB execution trials described here. 
9.4 Threats to validity 
This evaluation has important limitations: the evaluator developed several questions while seeing source data 
and code; the full-page gold anchors were selected from the texts rather than human-adjudicated; PDF text 
projection may omit layout or images; the original 4,113-row ledger is not available for direct row-level 
verification; the 3,962-edge transfer began with already structured relationships; the test grammar is narrower 
than open-language research; the assisted and external platforms were untested; some experiments use a 
small number of repeated queries; and the post hoc ablations had query-specific advantages. The private case 
corpus is a difficult application domain but is not statistically representative of all documents. No medical, legal 
or factual determinations about individuals are inferred from these software scores.

9.5 Evidence-order effects and evaluator reliability 
A retrospective audit of the initial comparison identified three observable errors: a critic’s earlier interpretation 
was treated as an enduring description of OOCC, source chronology and version succession were insufficiently 
examined, and the systems’ evidence was weighed asymmetrically [R1]. These failures demonstrate a practical 
requirement for comparative systems research: 
the reading order and rhetorical style of source material 
must not determine its evidentiary authority
. 
Primacy, anchoring, and authority cues offer plausible explanations for this pattern, although the internal causal 
mechanism was not experimentally identified. The distinction matters: the errors are documented in the 
evaluator’s outputs; the psychological or model-level explanation remains a hypothesis. To test for order effects, 
independent assessors could receive the same pinned materials under three randomized conditions—critic-first, 
target-first, and chronological source-first—while using an identical scoring rubric. Dated-fix errors, omitted 
source tests, misattributions, and changes in confidence would constitute measurable outcomes. 
This safeguard generalizes beyond the present comparison. Contemporaneous version records, source lineage, 
and disconfirming experiments must be consulted before an external criticism is promoted into a technical 
finding, particularly when a system has undergone several generations of development.

The most practical path forward preserves the existing source-authority machinery and introduces targeted 
extensions: precise refusal diagnostics, exact-field source routing, event candidates distinct from their 
documentary appearances, versioned correction relationships, and optional evidence-gated relation exploration. 
Those extensions admit concrete tests and provide McPhetridge with incremental opportunities to improve

documented history of tests, negative findings, and implementation changes. An external critique of one version

architectures turns comparative criticism into an engineering instrument rather than a contest of presentation or 
assertion.
