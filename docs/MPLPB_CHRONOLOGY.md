## What predates the November 14 critique

Swarm's August 30 tree includes origin/depth and profile gates; Networked's September 19 tree includes federation and provenance controls. Git blob comparisons found all 26 upstream Swarm Python files and all 28 Networked Python files byte-identical to the copies in current Combined. Smart Local matched 13 of 19 Python paths; six paths involving case normalization were unmatched, not verified different. [Per-file identities](../research/MPLPB_ECOSYSTEM_CODE_IDENTITY.json) retain the exact results. This is stronger evidence of source continuity than a README date alone. It does not establish general truth checking or a successful new upstream test run.

The November 10 pin cited by Ben already includes the general-answer pipeline, deterministic evidence graph, supersession policy and specialist coordinator. Several documents and the pipeline and relation-graph Python files are byte-identical between that pin and current Combined. Therefore even that older revision should not be described as only retrieving passages. [Old/current blob comparison](../research/MPLPB_FEATURE_CHRONOLOGY.json) distinguishes internal review dates from actual commit presence. The graph document labels itself proposed; source presence alone does not award every proposed behavior a pass.

The current `mplpb_work/source_support.py` is absent from the old pin. The current repair was merged November 15, after the stated November 14 paper date. The paired native tests show two historical gaps against the new probe criterion and seven current passes. They do not show that Ben had tested the repaired version or that his older observations were invalid.

## What the dates do not prove

Repository creation is not an independently authenticated first-publication date. Git author/committer timestamps and internal document dates have different evidentiary weight. These records do not prove intellectual priority, completeness, causal influence or deliberate version selection. The excerpt's proposed precision, occurrence-identity and general reconstruction extensions may still exceed implemented finite contracts. Ben's private test packages remain unavailable; his choice of pin cannot be explained from dates alone.
