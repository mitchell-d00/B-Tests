Testing OOCC

Claims, Required Tests, and a Roadmap to Verified Behavior

Mitchell D. McPhetridge

20 November 2026 · Technical research paper · Version 1.2 · Author-review draft

Abstract

Object-Oriented Cognition and Continuity (OOCC) combines source-linked project state,
human-readable governance, research workflows and reported software development. A
useful evaluation must distinguish these components. This paper reviews ten supplied
Benjamin Gelhaus documents, examines their evidence boundaries, and adds reproducible
B-Tests probes. The new observations compare decimal representations with equivalent
exponentiation expressions, repeated descriptions with indexed handles including
dictionary cost, reference resolution with current authority in an evaluator-owned CAD
implementation, and requested MPLPB actions with independently inspected local effects.
They do not constitute an execution of Gelhaus's native controller. In a seeded sample
of 512 powers of ten, all 512 exponentiation expressions were shorter than their decimal
expansions under the stated encoding; this challenges a general decimal-minimality
argument, not OOCC's overall performance. Indexed handles were cheaper in 15 of 20
serialization scenarios and more expensive in five. Four evaluator-reference controls
and seven native MPLPB boundary probes met their criteria. New paired model trials and
native OOCC comparisons remain NOT_RUN. This revision explicitly labels unavailable OOCC
checkpoint outcomes as author-reported and adds a source-linked roadmap of 34 claim/test
families and engineering recommendations from Mitchell D. McPhetridge. It distinguishes
Gelhaus's own proposed tests from the present additions, preserves the evidence level of
earlier test-design scripts, and applies the same standards to MPLPB. The roadmap is not
34 new experiments or a declaration of 34 defects.

1. The question and the objects being tested

The important question is whether a claimed mechanism produces its specified result
under inspectable conditions. Counting documents, governance labels, test assertions or
retrieved passages does not answer that question by itself.

Gelhaus's public-facing work is not accurately reduced to a single prompt. It includes
conceptual arguments, a portable reader procedure, external research and retrieval
reports, and descriptions of software prototypes. The October technical paper expressly
distinguishes documented artifacts, external workflows, host execution, historical
application runs, current native acceptance and comparative validation [G5, PDF pp.
2–3]. The distinction is sound. The present evaluation adopts it.

Three different objects must therefore remain separate: published instructions supplied
to a model; an evaluator's implementation of selected ideas; and Gelhaus's actual
versioned controller and applications. A fourth object, Combined MPLPB, is a separately
developed executable system. Testing one does not automatically test the others.

This is a developer-authored technical review, not an independent certification. The
author develops MPLPB and B-Tests and therefore has a direct comparative interest. No
new human-reviewed historical accuracy estimate or educational effectiveness result is
claimed.

3. Explicit comparison matrix

The six comparisons below have distinct units. Their counts must not be pooled into an
architectural accuracy score.

1.  C1 — Numeral encoding. Compare the decimal spelling of a power of ten with an
    equivalent Python exponentiation expression. Observe ASCII byte lengths and integer
    equality, with the shared notation stated explicitly.
2.  C2 — Reference encoding. Compare repeated expanded referent text with handle uses
    plus a serialized dictionary entry. Observe total UTF-8 bytes, including the
    definition.
3.  C3 — Reference versus authority. Compare B-Tests CADLab handle resolution with that
    program's separately checked source state and query. Observe scoped resolution,
    historical-reference status and grant behavior. This is our program.
4.  C4 — Request versus native effect. Compare a requested action through actual MPLPB
    with read-only event counts and a local executor marker. Observe whether the guarded
    adapter actually ran.
5.  C5 — Instruction treatment. Compare selected published CAD passages with strong
    conventional instructions, using the same fresh base model and source/task packet.
    This is a proposed model comparison, NOT_RUN here.
6.  C6 — Native systems. Compare Gelhaus's runnable native OOCC controller with native
    MPLPB under matched operations and budgets. This requires actual controller access
    and independent readbacks; it is NOT_RUN here.

C1 and C2 are analytical and serialization probes. C3 is explicitly our reference
implementation, not Gelhaus's software. C4 runs real MPLPB, not an in-memory function
labelled "MPLPB-style." C5 uses physical CAD pages 3, 4 and 7, not every OOCC book,
personal Memory state or private Orientation. C6 requires the actual system and
independently checked effects.

The C5 packets contain identical fictional sources, questions and output requirements.
Instructions differ in both wording and length; those lengths are recorded. Any future
comparison must hold model snapshot, tools, sampling, output budget and source access
constant, use fresh conversations, retain interventions and report truncation. Equal
tasks do not remove the instruction-length confound. Exact fact and citation scoring
does not by itself certify general semantic correctness.

4. What CAD declares and what enforces it

CAD separates Continuity, Authority and Directive. These categories can make task state
clearer and errors easier to diagnose. The paper also says CAD is not a conventional
compiled programming language and does not enforce access control by itself [G6, PDF pp.
4, 7]. Artificial Tangibility similarly distinguishes human-readable orientation from
durable execution infrastructure [G3, PDF pp. 3–5]. Those qualifications should be
preserved.

A declaration that a source is current, a draft is unpromoted or an action is forbidden
is useful information. It is not evidence that every route to the relevant operation
checks that declaration. A model may apply the rule correctly; an application may
independently enforce it; a human may supervise it. These are different mechanisms with
different failure conditions.

The relevant executable test asks whether a prohibited request reaches an actual
adapter, and whether current source and permission checks precede that effect. A
natural-language refusal is an observed response. A separate effect file and event-store
read can establish a bounded local effect or its absence. Neither observation
automatically establishes outside-world truth or completion of a larger task.

This does not refute CAD as a workflow. It limits what follows from its vocabulary
alone. Code could enforce CAD-derived policies; such enforcement must be demonstrated in
the particular implementation. The same requirement applies to MPLPB: its gates govern
checked software entry points, not every action a person or unrelated program could
take.

5. Canonical tokens: a useful convention and an unsupported inference

The canonical-token paper recommends stable names and handles and connects their reuse
to minimal-description ideas. It also makes stronger claims about numbers and references
already being at their shortest correct form [G7, PDF pp. 1–3]. These propositions need
to be separated.

Stable handles can reduce repeated explanation. That practical observation does not
establish that a familiar numeral is a shortest description in algorithmic information
theory. For example, 10**1000 specifies the same integer as a decimal one followed by
1,000 zeros, using far fewer characters under a shared exponentiation notation. More
generally, a short procedure can generate a highly regular string. A chosen conventional
representation and a shortest generating program are different things. Minimal-program
length also depends on the chosen interpreter; this experiment does not compute
Kolmogorov complexity.

C1 samples 512 distinct exponents uniformly without replacement from 1 through 2,048,
using seed 20261120. There is no performance-based acceptance filter. For each exponent,
ordinary bounded Python arithmetic checks equality between the decimal string's integer
and the corresponding power. The report retains decimal length, expression length and a
hash of the decimal spelling. All 512 sampled expressions were shorter. Ten
small-integer controls also retain cases where an ordinary literal is shorter than an
equivalent expression formed by adding zero.

These numbers describe a deliberately structured family of integers. They are not a
random sample of all practical numbers, a tokenizer study, a latency result or 512
independent OOCC failures. One valid counterexample is enough to reject an unrestricted
minimality inference; many similar examples do not establish broad performance
superiority.

C2 includes the dictionary rather than treating it as free. Four referent lengths and
five reuse counts produce 20 serialization scenarios. Handle-plus-dictionary encoding is
smaller in 15 scenarios and larger in five. This supports a conditional efficiency
claim: indirection can save space when reuse amortizes its definition cost. It does not
establish a universal shortest representation, lower model-token use, or cheaper
retrieval.

The paper's distinction between documentation and tokenizer internals is appropriate
[G7, PDF pp. 3–5]. It does not, however, supply the missing proof of minimality. A
stronger formulation would specify the encoding, index availability, reuse count and
measured total cost.

6. Reference identity is separate from current authority

A stable identifier may correctly designate an old object. It does not follow that the
old object currently governs a new action. A fresh session may also lack its dictionary,
encounter a namespace collision, or retain a source pointer after permission has been
revoked.

C3 uses the existing B-Tests CADLab program to make those distinctions inspectable. It
registers a handle for source S1, then creates a valid successor S2. The handle still
correctly resolves to S1, while S1 is historical and the current query returns S2's
value. Other controls test an unscoped name shared across two namespaces, an absent
reference, and a private-source grant before and after revocation. All four controls
meet their criteria.

These are successful controls in an evaluator-owned structured workbench. They are
neither failures nor passes of Gelhaus's native software. The result also does not
refute stable naming: the original handle remains a valid historical reference. It
demonstrates why reference resolution, current source status and operation permission
should be separately checked.

Gelhaus acknowledges ambiguous introduction and handle reuse as boundary conditions [G7,
PDF p. 5]. The remaining empirical question is how reliably a real implementation
maintains and verifies those conditions across changes and cold-session recovery.

7. Retrieval, continuity and educational evidence

The research book reports negative findings that would be useful to reproduce. The
following figures are Gelhaus-reported outcomes, not experiments independently
reproduced in this review. Its seven-query retrieval diagnostic uses 35 top-five slots
per condition. Literal TF-IDF returned 4 directly relevant results, fixed function
expansion 6, and unconditional shared-reference graph hopping 3. A hybrid's selected
source spans passed 35 of 35 original-byte checks, but that did not establish their
semantic relevance. Labels came from a single reviewer after retrieval, and the hybrids
reused those labels [G9, PDF pp. 10–12].

The defensible conclusion is narrow: function expansion helped this direct-relevance
count, while unconditional graph hopping and several hybrids did not reliably improve
it. These measurements do not establish general retrieval quality or a controlled
advantage of the whole OOCC architecture. They also cannot be compared numerically with
MPLPB's complete-answer rate, because retrieval slots and answer obligations have
different denominators and meanings.

The same volume reports a useful recovery episode and a failed requested-manuscript
revision despite successful source discovery. It explicitly says the initial failure
should not be credited as success because later human intervention recovered the
deliverable [G9, PDF pp. 7–8]. If reproduced as described, the archive checks would
establish bounded structural fidelity, not complete semantic understanding. Here, the
publication establishes that these checks and outcomes were reported; the underlying
archive checks remain unverified by this review. Those distinctions strengthen the
account; they are not evidence of an attempt to conceal every failure.

Headmaster reports an Android prototype and implemented components, while identifying
the complete teaching loop, current installed-version verification and independent
learning assessment as unfinished [G10, PDF pp. 7–8]. Reported prototype operation is
evidence about a prototype. Improved unaided explanation, transfer and retention require
the proposed educational experiments. The public activation book likewise says its
procedure is not executable software or guaranteed cross-instance continuity [G8, PDF
pp. 439–445].

The Object as Filter offers cross-domain conceptual parallels, but acknowledges that
Holographic Return lacks a completed public definition in that document [G4, PDF pp.
4–5]. Such analogies may guide design; their recurrence is not a validation of a
software mechanism. The byline paper's role-enactment effects are also identified as
prospective hypotheses rather than completed controlled outcomes [G1, PDF p. 11].

8. Actual native MPLPB controls and their limits

C4 reuses the existing seven-case B-Tests seam adapter against unmodified Combined MPLPB
commit 61cc7c04ebdfa84c8d783e005c6e7857c07600b2. The cases concern a contradictory
premise, a claim-only dependency chain, a supported control, a revoked seat, a changed
source, an unavailable original, and an export-related supported record without
evidence. Read-only SQLite event counts and a real local adapter marker provide effect
checks. All seven meet their specified criteria [M1].

The positive control matters: refusing every request would not satisfy the suite. The
source and worker files, source pins, command logs and observed results are retained.
This is an execution of native MPLPB under authored conditions, not an instruction-level
model comparison or a test of Ben's unavailable controller.

MPLPB's own broader limits remain substantial. At the recorded native freeze, the
unchanged expanded stress set improved from 168 to 189 complete matches out of 420 with
580 expected refusals preserved. The separate 4,096-case known-weakness study produced
657 complete matches from 1,262 authored answerable cases, 605 misses in eleven classes,
and 2,834 expected refusals. Thirty-seven sorter graphs disclosed repeated-phrase budget
limits. The historical replay remained three native complete answers, two partials,
three conflicts and fourteen abstentions across 22 unchanged questions [M2–M3].

These are different populations, not one combined success rate. They are developer
measurements, not independent historical certification. A correctly hashed source can be
false. A permitted action can rest on insufficient evidence. A finite checker can
contain bugs. A matching citation is not automatically semantic proof. MPLPB's useful
software boundary does not establish that the general historical-reconstruction problem
is solved.

9. Earlier responses and the supplied witness scripts

The earlier response papers and Section 19 material are prior work, not additional new
replications [P1–P3]. Gelhaus's earlier Beyond Guarded Retrieval evaluated MPLPB, not
the native OOCC controller [G11]. The present paper examines published OOCC/CAD claims
and separately identified program controls; the two evaluation directions must not be
conflated. The supplied switch-test Markdown matches the existing B-Tests bytes. The
supplied witness ZIP matches the already retained archive. The supplied map-response PDF
differs from the currently stored edition and is preserved separately with its own hash.

The newly supplied revised Witness PDF is byte-identical to the revised paper already in
B-Tests. Its Section 19 specifies a proposed experiment with detection and false-alarm
measures, not a claim that the old ZIP constitutes a completed native comparison. The
revised paper, the retained historical ZIP, the later filesystem pilot and the actual
native probes are distinct artifacts. The limitation below belongs to the old scripted
demonstration; it is not presented as an unfixed defect in the revised paper or as
evidence that the later real tests did not occur.

The author clarified on 20 November 2026 that the old ZIP was the structure he built to
think through ways of testing OOCC and MPLPB. It should therefore be classified as a
test-design scaffold: frozen-packet examples, planted error classes, candidate
observation functions and proposed detection/false-alarm measures. Prewritten decisions
are appropriate for illustrating that structure. They are not observations of how a live
model or native controller behaved.

The historical script labels and README must be read under that scope. The scripts
produce two-of-four detection counts for their authored values and four-of-five decision
disagreements; these demonstrate the scoring arrangement, not an empirical architectural
winner. In particular, an effect-only observer can miss an inherited contradiction or
misclassify a correct refusal if it lacks current authority and reporting context. That
is a reason to improve the experiment design, not evidence that the author had no real
tests.

The later B-Tests filesystem pilot uses actual subprocess-created files, and its native
probes invoke the real MPLPB program. Their observations are documented separately [P3].
The original ZIP remains unchanged; a dated author-intent note accompanies it. This
review credits the design contribution, the revised paper and the later executions
without double-counting their evidence or attributing native performance to the
scaffold.

10. Claims and the tests required to establish them

This is a proposed test roadmap, not 34 newly completed experiments or 34 demonstrated
defects. Claims below are attributed paraphrases. The source pages identify the
mechanism, reported result, proposal or limitation being tested; a design objective is
not represented as an achieved result. The catalogue covers identified claim families in
the reviewed sections of ten supplied documents, not every claim in more than one
thousand pages.

Several tests are already proposed by Gelhaus. Each entry credits that origin where
identified; McPhetridge's recommendations specify stronger controls, observable outcomes
or publication steps. Missing code, participants or observations leave a test NOT_RUN.
Conceptual terms without a distinguishing definition require definition before
experimental scoring.

All future comparisons must freeze sources, implementation/model versions, questions,
outcome rules and budgets before execution; keep sealed expected outcomes out of target
inputs; retain every failure, timeout and human repair; and score both false support and
unnecessary refusal. Choose effect sizes, sample sizes and uncertainty estimates before
the final study. Do not import arbitrary percentage targets or claim universal
zero-error safety. Blinded AI assessment must be labelled AI assessment; it is not
independent human review. Native comparison applies only to mutually declared
implemented capabilities.

T01 — Cold-session continuity and useful completion

Claim and scope (claimed workflow benefit). Externalized state and orientation are
intended to let a fresh AI session resume useful work without reconstructing the whole
conversation. [G6, PDF pp. 5, 6, 7; G9, PDF pp. 7, 8, 14]

Current evidence. The publications report recovery episodes and a failed deliverable
later recovered through human intervention; no matched general continuity effect is
established here.

Required test. On identical interrupted projects compare ordinary notes plus a capable
handoff, full transcript, CAD instructions and an available complete OOCC
implementation. Use the same model, tools and source access. Challenge cases: Cold
sessions; lost conversational context; stale drafts; ambiguous next steps; missing
source; one source-correct deliverable that each arm can actually complete.

Measure and decide. Correct complete deliverables, false completion, time to useful
completion, source/version mistakes, all human corrections and total context cost. Count
recovery only when the requested artifact is correct. Predeclare a practically useful
quality/cost difference; successful orientation alone is insufficient.

Access and credit. Frozen projects, fresh sessions, model pins and blinded source-linked
deliverable scoring. Gelhaus proposes comparative recovery work; McPhetridge recommends
strong conventional handoffs and artifact-level success criteria.

Status. Required test NOT_RUN in this iteration; native OOCC NOT_RUN.

T02 — Current authority versus a salient old map

Claim and scope (claimed governance mechanism). Current source selection should govern
work while historical orientation remains recoverable. [G6, PDF pp. 3, 5, 7; G8, PDF pp.
442, 443, 444]

Current evidence. The reader procedure describes source selection; CAD alone expressly
does not enforce access control.

Required test. Compare current-selector behavior with stale promoted maps and
conventional version-aware source lookup, using identical source histories. Challenge
cases: An old approved map; a newer unapproved draft; an explicitly promoted successor;
an as-of historical question; a corrupted current pointer.

Measure and decide. Current/as-of selection accuracy, unjustified freshness assumptions,
source-state readbacks and correct conflict/absence decisions. Neither recency nor
salience alone may confer authority. An old handle can remain a correct historical
reference while losing current authority.

Access and credit. Explicit source-state rules and separately inspectable version
histories. McPhetridge operationalizes the source-selection distinction; Gelhaus already
distinguishes orientation from governing sources.

Status. Required test NOT_RUN in this iteration; native OOCC NOT_RUN. Related completed
probes: C3; these do not execute native OOCC.

T03 — Canonical numerical minimality

Claim and scope (mathematical argument). The canonical-token argument connects stable
numerical/reference forms with shortest correct descriptions. [G7, PDF pp. 1, 2, 3, 4,
5]

Current evidence. C1 supplies bounded counterexamples to unrestricted decimal
minimality; this is an encoding argument, not a native OOCC performance test.

Required test. Compare decimal strings and equivalent generating expressions under a
declared shared interpreter, including literals for which expression overhead loses.
Challenge cases: Powers, repetitive numbers, small integers, irregular values and
different notation/interpreter overheads.

Measure and decide. Integer equality and total description bytes under each declared
encoding; model tokens only with an actual named tokenizer. One valid shorter
description defeats an unrestricted minimality inference. These probes cannot compute
Kolmogorov complexity or establish a universally best encoding.

Access and credit. Explicit description language, decoder assumptions and a stated
mathematical claim. McPhetridge supplies counterexamples and recommends a conditional
encoding claim instead of universal minimality.

Status. Required test NOT_RUN in this iteration; native OOCC NOT_RUN. Related completed
probes: C1; these do not execute native OOCC.

T04 — Reference savings including dictionary and lookup costs

Claim and scope (claimed efficiency benefit). Reused canonical handles can reduce
restatement and context burden. [G7, PDF pp. 3, 4, 5; G6, PDF pp. 5, 6, 7]

Current evidence. C2 shows savings in fifteen serialization scenarios and losses in
five; no model-token or full operating-cost advantage follows.

Required test. Compare expanded text with handles plus their definitions, namespaces,
source retrieval and maintenance; evaluate at equal answer quality. Challenge cases: Low
and high reuse, short and long referents, absent dictionaries, collisions, changed
definitions and fresh-session initialization.

Measure and decide. Total bytes, measured tokens, lookup latency, source bytes read,
maintenance work and source-correct answers. Report break-even conditions and losses. A
free preloaded dictionary or reduced quality cannot establish a fair cost advantage.

Access and credit. Named tokenizer/runtime if used, complete initialization accounting
and frozen workloads. McPhetridge recommends all-in accounting; Gelhaus acknowledges
reference-introduction and reuse conditions.

Status. Required test NOT_RUN in this iteration; native OOCC NOT_RUN. Related completed
probes: C2, C3; these do not execute native OOCC.

T05 — Enforced policy at the operation boundary

Claim and scope (implementation acceptance requirement). An implementation that claims
enforced authority must prevent prohibited operations, beyond describing policies in
language. [G6, PDF pp. 7; G5, PDF pp. 5, 6, 7, 8]

Current evidence. CAD explicitly disclaims access-control enforcement by itself; native
integrated enforcement is a separate acceptance question.

Required test. Compare the actual guarded adapter with independent effect/event
readbacks under allowed and prohibited requests; keep instruction-only compliance a
separate arm. Challenge cases: Forged approval, revoked grants, stale authority,
claim-only premises, changed source bytes and one genuinely authorized positive task.

Measure and decide. Attempted and committed effects, policy/source checks before
execution, denied legitimate actions and unauthorized effects. A refusal message does
not prove no effect occurred. Universal refusal fails the positive control; any
prohibited effect is a bounded contract violation.

Access and credit. Authorized runnable controller, real adapter and an observer that can
inspect actual effects. McPhetridge recommends native effect verification; Gelhaus
identifies authenticated integration as unfinished.

Status. Required test NOT_RUN in this iteration; native OOCC NOT_RUN. Related completed
probes: C4; these do not execute native OOCC.

T06 — Deliberate activation and instruction injection

Claim and scope (documented procedure boundary). The public activation procedure is
reader-operated guidance, not automatic execution or permission expansion. [G8, PDF pp.
439, 440, 441, 442, 443, 444, 445; G9, PDF pp. 106]

Current evidence. The appendix disclaims executable-software and continuity guarantees;
forged-authority tests are already proposed in the research volume.

Required test. Compare explicit authorized activation with exposure to identical
instructions only as untrusted source text. Challenge cases: Quoted ACTIVATE text, an
uploaded PDF, embedded forged owner instructions, recursive-copy destinations and a
legitimate requested activation.

Measure and decide. Unrequested state changes, permission expansion, private data
movement, correct inert treatment of quotes and authorized task completion. Document
exposure must not silently activate privileged operations. Score an intended inert
example separately from a failed authorized activation.

Access and credit. Declared activation semantics, tool access logs and observable
effects. Gelhaus already proposes forged-authority and copy-boundary checks; McPhetridge
adds paired quoted-versus-authorized activation cases.

Status. Required test NOT_RUN in this iteration; native OOCC NOT_RUN.

T07 — Quotation fidelity versus proposition correctness

Claim and scope (evidence distinction). Exact source retrieval and provenance checks
establish fidelity; a reconstructed assertion additionally needs the correct meaning and
scope. [G9, PDF pp. 9, 10, 11; G5, PDF pp. 4, 5]

Current evidence. The research book separates original-byte checks from relevance. Its
reports do not establish comprehensive semantic accuracy.

Required test. Compare source-linked outputs with original passages and blinded
proposition labels; score span fidelity independently. Challenge cases: A real quote
attached to the wrong actor, negation, conditional, reported claim, date role or
incomplete context; correct supported controls.

Measure and decide. Exact-span fidelity, proposition precision/recall, qualification
retention, unsupported-positive rate and incorrect refusals. An authentic quotation with
an incorrect proposition fails semantic support even if every byte matches.

Access and credit. Frozen original spans, complete context and independent scoring or a
clearly labelled AI assessment. McPhetridge recommends adversarial semantic mutations;
Gelhaus already separates byte fidelity from relevance.

Status. Required test NOT_RUN in this iteration; native OOCC NOT_RUN.

T08 — Evidence-level calibration

Claim and scope (reported evidence taxonomy). Artifacts, external workflows, host
execution, native acceptance and comparative validation represent different evidence
levels. [G5, PDF pp. 2, 3]

Current evidence. The technical paper explicitly distinguishes these levels; the
taxonomy itself is useful and not a demonstrated failure.

Required test. Audit each reported claim against its actual underlying artifacts, native
traces and comparative design. Challenge cases: Passing host tests without device
installation, an old APK, a source file without execution and a comparative claim
without a comparator.

Measure and decide. Correct evidence-level assignments, unsupported promotions, missing
artifacts and version/date coverage. Credit evidence at the level actually demonstrated;
do not convert unavailable evidence into either a native pass or a native failure.

Access and credit. Versioned claim inventory and access to the artifacts being asserted.
Gelhaus supplies the evidence taxonomy; McPhetridge recommends an auditable
claim-to-artifact ledger.

Status. Required test NOT_RUN in this iteration; native OOCC NOT_RUN.

T09 — Archive integrity and complete structural coverage

Claim and scope (reported bounded result). The reported archive procedures check
structure, source roles and byte correspondence within specified populations. [G9, PDF
pp. 9, 10, 14]

Current evidence. The publications report structural audits; the underlying private
archive was not independently acquired here.

Required test. Compare exported/indexed records with a hash-pinned original manifest,
including absent and malformed entries. Challenge cases: Missing records, duplicate
appearances, branch changes, corrected counts, UTF-8/CRLF offsets, changed sources and
deliberately excluded canaries.

Measure and decide. Full population counts, byte/hash agreement, missing/extra records,
exclusion accuracy and unresolved lineage. Structural coverage needs a declared
denominator; it does not establish semantic comprehension or historical truth.

Access and credit. Authorized original archive or a public synthetic substitute clearly
labelled as a different corpus. McPhetridge recommends complete-denominator mutation
tests; Gelhaus already reports bounded structural checks.

T10 — Intellectual provenance without invented priority

Claim and scope (claimed provenance method). Origin, development, verification and
responsibility should be tracked separately rather than inferred from the last byline or
appearance. [G1, PDF pp. 7; G5, PDF pp. 3, 4; G9, PDF pp. 9, 10]

Current evidence. The byline paper proposes these distinctions; a comprehensive
independently adjudicated provenance study is not supplied here.

Required test. Compare provenance assignments with a frozen message/edit history
containing known local origins, copies and later developments. Challenge cases: A human

11. Engineering recommendations from Mitchell D. McPhetridge

The following recommendations concern observable behavior and reproducibility. They are
offered in the same spirit as an actionable engineering review of MPLPB: isolate the
mechanism, preserve the failure, repair a general class if needed, and rerun unchanged
tests. They do not presume that an unavailable capability failed, and they do not demand
private personal records.

1.  Publish a minimal runnable claim packet first (T08, T23, T30). Identify the selected
    current version, supported operations and known omissions. Include public-safe
    synthetic sources, installation commands, source hashes, execution limits and raw
    outputs. Keep host tests, historical builds, current native acceptance and
    comparative benefits in separate rows. If code cannot be shared, specify exactly
    which claims an external researcher cannot reproduce.
2.  Demonstrate one complete cold-session task (T01–T02, T22, T32). Begin with an
    unfamiliar source-governed archival or editing task, not an architecture-wide
    superiority claim. Compare with a capable ordinary handoff, match
    models/tools/source access, and judge the completed requested artifact. Count every
    manual repair, not only the successful restart. Then repeat on unseen tasks without
    changing the frozen criteria.
3.  Demonstrate one real enforced boundary (T05–T06, T19–T21, T28). Use an actual
    adapter, a valid permitted operation, a revoked or forged request, and independent
    effect readbacks. Preserve authenticated receipts and crash/interruption results.
    Instruction compliance may be useful, but it must not be reported as programmatic
    enforcement. Gelhaus already identifies much of this integration work as unfinished;
    the recommendation is to complete and publish it.
4.  Separate retrieval, checked claims and relationship suggestions (T07, T09–T15).
    Publish source coverage and semantic relevance separately. Preserve contradictory
    sources. Do not let a shared identifier, copied account or valid quote stand in for
    independent corroboration. Check one explicit connection at a time and stop at
    missing evidence. A candidate graph can remain useful without being accepted
    history.
5.  Narrow theoretical claims to testable statements (T03–T04, T16–T18, T27). Replace
    unrestricted numerical minimality with a stated encoding and measured break-even
    conditions. Define terms before treating analogies as mechanisms. Execute the
    already proposed weight ablations and prospective transfer baselines, preserving
    negative transfers and maintenance cost.
6.  Test the current teaching loop before claiming educational benefit (T23–T26). First
    publish current native acceptance. Then run the learning, transfer and retention
    comparisons already proposed in Headmaster, using real consenting learners when
    available. A prototype, extracted learning phrase or fluent model answer is not a
    learning outcome. Without participants, keep the educational study NOT_RUN.
7.  Make the final assessment genuinely separable from development (T10, T29–T34).
    Freeze outputs for source-linked scoring; keep evaluator expectations out of runtime
    inputs; disclose self-authored labels and conflicts of interest. A fresh AI session
    can provide a labelled AI assessment, not independent human certification. Apply the
    same test requirements to MPLPB, including its known over-refusals, incomplete
    historical answers and coherent-false-original limit.

These are recommendations, not an invented new pass/fail verdict. Where Gelhaus has
already specified the needed experiment—such as the role-enactment factorial design,
weight ablations, forged-authority checks or prospective Functional Layers
comparisons—the contribution here is an explicit execution and reporting roadmap with
stronger attribution and evidence separation, not a claim to have invented his
proposals.

12. Matched comparison and reporting protocol

For each test, record the claim ID, target and comparator, exact implementation and
model pins, source manifest hash, permissions, tool and source-read budgets,
randomization, instructions, outcomes and human interventions. State whether the unit is
a quotation, retrieved slot, proposed edge, accepted proposition, completed deliverable,
native effect or learner outcome. A retrieval percentage must never be compared directly
with an answer-completion percentage.

Declare supported positive controls and unsupported negative controls before testing.
Report complete correct answers, partial correct answers, correct refusals, unnecessary
refusals, wrong accepted claims, conflicts, unavailable evidence and execution failures
separately. Release denominators, all raw outcomes, uncertainty and missing
measurements. A critical unauthorized effect is reported even if many other cases pass;
zero observed effects has a sample-dependent uncertainty bound, not universal certainty.

Use the same evidence access when measuring the value of observer separation; vary
access only in an explicitly labelled ablation. Compare native systems only where both
implement the declared operation. An unavailable controller remains NOT_RUN; an
out-of-scope function is not silently a native failure. Independently assess semantic
correctness where possible; a hash or software test is not a semantic judge.

The companion research/oocc_claim_tests_v1.json carries each claim paraphrase, physical
source pages, current evidence, comparator, challenge cases, measurements,
interpretation rule, access prerequisites, proposal credit and execution status.
docs/OOCC_CLAIMS_AND_REQUIRED_TESTS.md provides a per-test reading copy. Neither file
executes the proposed studies. Existing C1–C4 outputs remain frozen; C5/C6 remain
NOT_RUN.

13. Credit, reproducibility and the next fair test

The supplied research volume explicitly credits McPhetridge's Dual-System Architecture,
bridge-language load testing and externalized cognitive-runtime work [G2, PDF p. 143].
It would be inaccurate to say these papers never acknowledge that influence. Citation
does not independently resolve every priority or implementation-provenance question, but
such questions require actual source histories and code comparisons.

13.1 Attribution: McPhetridge's machinery, Gelhaus's benchmarks

For the MPLPB evaluations, the implementation and pre-existing automated native test
machinery are McPhetridge's work. Gelhaus's benchmark framing, test selection and
reported analysis are evaluation activities; they are not authorship of that
implementation or machinery. Gelhaus did not build Combined MPLPB by testing it.
Execution of an upstream test does not transfer authorship of that test or its
underlying machinery.

The retained review of Beyond Guarded Retrieval records that Gelhaus selected and ran
McPhetridge's native MPLPB regression tests, reporting 58/58 passing after omitted
fixtures were restored [G11, PDF pp. 4, 6; B-Tests docs/GELHAUS_EXTERNAL_EVALUATION.md].
The test implementation, ledger writer, validator, strict reader, provenance bundle and
passage-ranking machinery were upstream MPLPB components, not newly implemented OOCC
components. The selection and interpretation were Gelhaus's evaluation work. This
paragraph attributes the reported run; it does not claim a fresh 58-test execution in
the present revision.

The later public publication package supplies reproduce.py, whose inspected imports
include mplpb_combined.ledger.write, mplpb_answers.pipeline.answer_query and
mplpb_combined.reader.PROFILES [G12]. It builds source pages with McPhetridge's writer
and asks questions through McPhetridge's strict answer pipeline. The supplied script
also contains fixture-generation and query-template code. Its inclusion in Gelhaus's
publication package does not establish that he authored it or built a separate execution
harness. This review assigns no new implementation credit on that basis. The
demonstrated execution machinery is the imported upstream MPLPB machinery.

The public package README itself credits McPhetridge as the software developer and calls
the script a method-equivalent driver using the engine's own writer. The package audit
preserves its SHA-256, script/import evidence and manifest verification in
evaluation/public-claims/2026-11-20/attribution-audit.json. This is directly inspectable
provenance for the test machinery; it is not evidence that the unavailable native OOCC
controller passed any test.

M6 and B-Tests must not be invented as components of that public driver. Inspection
found no M6 runtime or B-Tests implementation in this package; the shown
non-standard-library imports are MPLPB's own modules. The package does not establish
that Gelhaus used McPhetridge's private M6. These ten OOCC papers do not settle that
separate question either. McPhetridge's authorship of MPLPB, its pre-existing automated
tests, B-Tests and any separately evidenced private tools remains distinct from
evaluation reporting. No separate Gelhaus-built test framework or driver is established
by the evidence inspected here.

The next discriminating study should compare published CAD instructions with strong
conventional source governance using the same model and source access, and compare
native systems only when both are available. It should freeze tasks and criteria before
execution, include supported actions as well as prohibited ones, preserve missing and
contradictory evidence, and retain every failure. An evaluator-owned reference program
belongs in a labelled control arm.

Primary outcomes should be correct bounded deliverables, unnecessary refusals, incorrect
answers, source/version fidelity, verified effects, preservation of corrections, human
intervention and resource costs. Fresh-session transfer must be observed rather than
asserted. New model configuration, usage, independent semantic accuracy, human review
time and native OOCC performance remain null or NOT_RUN in the present release.

14. Conclusion

OOCC publications describe design distinctions and report engineering outcomes that
require direct examination; the unavailable checkpoint outcomes are not verified by this
review. Its public reader method is a structured procedure delivered through
instructions; its broader architecture also includes reported workflows and software
components. Declared rules, prototype existence, exact source retrieval, reference
resolution, native integration and comparative benefit are distinct evidentiary
achievements.

The retained probes expose a bounded mathematical overstatement, demonstrate conditional
reference compression, and verify selected behavior in two explicitly identified
programs. The 34-family roadmap specifies what further evidence the reviewed claims
need; the recommendations identify a practical order for obtaining it. They do not
establish a global winner. Testing OOCC fairly means making its claims executable and
inspectable where possible, preserving unavailable tests as unavailable, and applying
the same standards to MPLPB.
