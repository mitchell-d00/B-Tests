WHEN THE MAP BECOMES THE AUTHORITY

A Critical Examination of OOCC, the CAD Control Grammar, and the Limits of Relational Unification

Mitchell D. McPhetridge

Research draft: 16 November 2026

Based on an initial draft dated 15 November 2026

AI-assisted preparation, execution and analysis. This is a critical research draft, not
independent peer review or certification of either architecture. The author develops
MPLPB, which Gelhaus has criticized. That competing interest is disclosed. No
independent human adjudication of the new model outputs is claimed.

Abstract

Object-Oriented Cognition and Continuity (OOCC) proposes external project structures
through which discontinuous AI sessions can recover work, distinguish governing material
and continue under declared authority. Continuity–Authority–Directive (CAD) supplies a
plain-language control grammar. This paper examines a specific risk: a human-approved
but false source may survive archival promotion, acquire unsupported relationships, and
pass through compressed handoffs as established project truth. It distinguishes that
hypothesis from a demonstrated failure and from the impossible demand to recover an
unobserved fact from an identical, misleading record alone.

Thirty real fresh-agent target sessions were executed across five authored synthetic
conditions, using published CAD excerpts and conventional source governance. In a
64-record, 5,120-relation-appearance benchmark, both treatments returned correct values
for all 119 requested questions and selected the warranted local action. Both also
discovered an unannounced contradiction in an accessible archive. In three-stage
propagation, a false original remained the basis for local P0 release requests, but
neither treatment inflated one observation root into independent corroboration or
accepted unsupported causal edges. In a single-project human export, CAD deferred
release pending validation evidence, whereas the conventional session requested release
from the inherited report. Both marked that report as unverified inheritance. CAD
exceeded one earlier compression budget and added one unrequested answer; the
conventional treatment exceeded two compression budgets. Several original source-set and
literal-string scores were overprescriptive and are retained with qualifications.

These findings neither falsify OOCC/CAD overall nor prove that inherited-error
reinforcement never occurs. They demonstrate a wrong-source persistence risk, bounded
task failures, and counterexamples to categorical claims that CAD cannot challenge
human-approved facts. A static audit of current MPLPB identifies a separate evidence
level: executable source, lineage and work-state checks within specified finite
contracts. Such checks are stronger than requested behavior for those contracts, but do
not certify the truth of every eligible source. Functional Layers forecasting and Ben
Gelhaus's live system were not evaluated. Shared relational vocabulary remains
distinguishable from demonstrated causal or predictive transfer.

Keywords: OOCC; CAD; human approval; provenance; source governance; inherited error;
compression; handoff; Functional Layers; MPLPB; falsifiability.

1. The question is authority inflation, not whether relationships are useful

A project can retain information while losing the reasons for trusting it. A later
runtime may recover a conclusion, its approval and its dependencies without recovering
the original qualification or failed attempt. A polished continuation can conceal that
loss. The concern is particularly consequential when a preserved interpretation becomes
a premise for action.

This paper calls the proposed failure mechanism the armored-worldview hypothesis. It is
a hypothesis about information handling, not a characterization of Gelhaus. Its proposed
sequence is: an erroneous original becomes a project fact; descendants inherit it; those
descendants are treated as corroboration; compression removes qualifications; and later
sessions continue under the strengthened interpretation. A demonstrated instance would
require tracing those transitions in actual outputs or state, not merely observing that
the architecture uses relational terminology.

The hypothesis constrains the tests in both directions. They must permit errors to
persist, unsupported relationships to gain authority, and correction to fail. They must
also permit the hypothesis to lose support when the system retains scope, challenges
approval, or stops affected work. A test designed to award only unfavorable outcomes is
not falsification.

A second distinction is essential. If the sole available original falsely reports an
equipment state, and no observation reveals the error, preserving that report is
different from inventing independent confirmation. Identical visible records can coexist
with different outside-world states. The evaluator cannot legitimately demand that a
model distinguish those states without information. It can ask whether source reports
retain provenance and uncertainty, and whether downstream claims exceed their support.

2. What Gelhaus's publications actually claim

CAD separates what must survive, what governs, and what action is permissible [1,
physical PDF pp. 1–4]. The paper describes a policy structure conveyed through language,
rather than claiming that three words alone solve governance. Its proposed evaluation
includes re-entry, source-current distinction, context burden, safe action, error
catchability, handoff quality and human burden [1, pp. 6–7]. These are operational
claims requiring comparison, not self-validating definitions.

Its limitations are explicit: “CAD is not a complete safety system” and “does not
guarantee factual correctness,” prevent all hallucination, or enforce access control by
itself [1, p. 7]. Stale objects and inaccurate authority labels can reproduce confusion.
A critique that attributes guaranteed truth or native enforcement to this paper would
strengthen its claim beyond what it states.

Artificial Tangibility describes bounded objects carrying role, authority, state,
boundary, relation, receipt, weight and next safe action. Its companion paper separates
artifact production from promotion; the human or declared authority structure determines
what becomes canonical or governing [2, pp. 4–6]. This is a substantive authorization
distinction. It does not make the promoted content true.

The October 26 v0.16 synthesis reports source-linked work, historical app records and
bounded modules while identifying unfinished integration [3, pp. 5–9]. It also states
that copies, similar phrases and derived summaries cannot count as independent
confirmations [3, p. 4]. Salience is distinct from evidence confidence [3, pp. 4–5].
These are directly relevant constraints on the proposed reinforcing cycle, not
admissions that the cycle necessarily occurs.

Chronology matters. An October mobile-controller integration boundary is not a verdict
about a later cloud workflow. The November reading edition describes human-invoked
multi-session work and distinguishes it from unattended agents [4, pp. 3–6]. It marks
written-contract autonomous continuity as not established, structural matches as
insufficient for historical causation, and controlled comparative recovery benefit as
not yet established [4, pp. 389–390]. These are reported evidence boundaries, not
experiments independently reproduced here.

No access was available to Gelhaus's current private Memory/Drive/SQL environment,
controller, scheduler, recipient records or original research packages. This paper
evaluates selected published instructions and supplied text; it does not claim an
exhaustive audit of every page of all supplied books.

3. Governance, documentary support and factual truth

Three questions must remain distinct:

1.  Who may change the project or authorize an action?
2.  Which record and version govern the procedure or current working state?
3.  What does the evidence establish about the proposition itself?

A human can possess the first authority and legitimately select the second record while
being wrong about the third question. Conversely, a correct observation may arrive
without permission to execute anything. Promotion is not an experiment, source integrity
is not source truth, and persistence is not independent support.

The relevant criticism is therefore narrower than “the human gate makes everything
false.” A gate can prevent unauthorized mutation while admitting a mistaken premise. The
empirical question is whether later processing preserves that premise only as an
attributed report, or increases its authority without new evidence.

An enforced boundary and an instruction are different mechanisms. Software can reject a
transition before an adapter executes it under a specified contract. A model can select
no action because it followed instructions. Both can be useful, but a successful
instance of the latter is not proof of the former. The new tests persist model
selections before evaluation; they do not install a stronger truth checker in the CAD
arm or rewrite its choices.

4. Methods: five seams and thirty target sessions

The evaluation used the published CAD packet already retained in B-Tests: CAD physical
PDF pages 1–7 and the publication-safe editing contract from No More First Dates With
AI, physical PDF page 407 [1, 5]. Its SHA-256 is
95658d6e997b8d36af446d9bf86024fd16937b7885daf31808c23dba27a11af8. The conventional
packet requests ordinary source governance, correction, uncertainty, permissions and
recoverable rejected alternatives; its SHA-256 is
27f437c36acf0979b708ee341c441038a1687d6d493b008c81b1bc4f2ab1727d. Neither is represented
as Gelhaus's complete current operating environment.

Target agents started with fork_turns=none, without the parent discussion, other
treatments or outcome keys. The same inherited agent-model configuration was used; the
interface did not expose its exact snapshot or sampling settings. Instructions could
reveal the treatment. Operators and semantic review were unblinded, and filesystem
isolation relied on instructions rather than an OS boundary.

The five conditions address source-to-fact, fact-to-relation, archive-to-handoff,
receipt-to-action, and evaluator-to-finding transitions. Source bytes, expected outcomes
or prospective stage recipes were frozen locally before dispatch. There was no trusted
public preregistration timestamp. All cases are evaluator-authored synthetic development
material, not independently authored holdouts.

The public records retain actual input content, normalized final JSON values, frozen
specifications, selected effects, hashes and original scores. Original collaboration
messages remain authoritative; the public archives are not raw provider response bytes
or complete exported tool transcripts. Some submission transcript hashes identify the
normalized final-answer archive, a limitation explicitly recorded in provenance.
Scripted fault controls are never labeled target-model outputs.

Thirty sessions are not thirty independent project samples. Sixteen observations belong
to four earlier chains, six to two later chains, and the remaining eight are
single-stage target sessions across three conditions. No population reliability or
statistical equivalence claim follows from these counts.

4.1 Simplified correction and handoff check: sixteen sessions

Four trajectories paired CAD and conventional governance on a false approved map and a
legitimate-release control. Each used four fresh stages with 2,200, 1,400, 900 and
500-character handoff limits. Two or three short originals were supplied afresh each
stage. A later correction revoked permission and blocked the task. This was a simplified
instruction check, not independent or live-system validation.

Both treatments corrected the false test result, retained F0's failure and a rejected
relationship, preserved sandbox scope, and selected no release after revocation. Both
selected legitimate release before revocation. CAD's final positive-control handoff used
503 characters against 500; the conventional false-map handoff used 501. Those are
observed budget failures, not evidence of fidelity at the specified compression limit.

Some handoffs abbreviated source identifiers. That is measurable loss of full identifier
strings, but the catalogue and originals remained available. The study did not establish
unrecoverable provenance loss or summary-only fidelity.

4.2 Relational saturation and source decisions: two sessions

A frozen seed generated 64 documentary records, ten projects, 5,120 planning-relation
appearances and 119 questions. Copied promoted maps asserted portfolio-wide success.
Original records supplied numeric thresholds, sandbox restrictions, a partial permission
correction, failed work, causal controls, independent dataset counts, worker grants and
reading acknowledgements.

Both treatments returned the correct values for all 119 requested questions and selected
P7 alone for local release. CAD produced an unrequested Q120. Its handoff used 881
characters against 900; the conventional handoff used exactly 900. The 5,120 appearances
are not 5,120 independent facts, and the generated records are not 64 typeset research
pages. Shared templates limit semantic difficulty.

4.3 Unannounced archive contradiction: four sessions

A human-approved high-weight map said Caldera was active. The target was asked to
continue ordinary work without being warned that the map was wrong. It could search an
index of 64 archived records. The false-map condition contained a current register
saying inactive; the positive control contained an active register.

Both treatments found the current original, contested the wrong map and selected no
release. Both selected release in the valid control. A separate Linux inotify monitor
observed current-primary file access in all four sessions and stopped before evaluator
source reads. Opening is not proof of comprehension or provider identity, but it is
stronger evidence than an unverified read claim alone.

This demonstrates possible checking without the operator identifying the error. It does
not test a false original without contrary evidence. The original was readily locatable,
and the project human's unawareness was simulated, not measured in a human-participant
study.

4.4 False-original archival propagation: six sessions

One original, OBS-01, falsely reported activity during P0's local check. The hidden
physical state was inactive; no target-accessible record revealed this. Human-promoted
descendants claimed readiness across ten projects and causal influence. Their metadata
accurately identified OBS-01 as their common root. There were 10, 100 and 1,000
descendants across three stages, with 1,200, 700 and 400-character handoffs. The
original disappeared after stage zero. Actual saved structured state and handoff both
transferred.

Both treatments retained one root, limited documentary support to P0, accepted no causal
edges, and requested only P0 release at every stage. Both retained the warning that
outside-world status was unverified and did not claim a new read of the unavailable
original. CAD's handoffs used 859, 584 and 384 characters. The conventional handoffs
used 911, 627 and 410; its last failed the 400-character limit.

The false P0 report nevertheless survived and remained operationally usable. Those
requests are wrong relative to the constructed hidden physical state. The observed
limitation is that source-bounded continuity can preserve an error and support action on
it. The hypothesized inflation into independent confirmations or supported downstream
causal claims did not occur. Original-error detection is INCONCLUSIVE because the
necessary distinguishing observation was unavailable.

The structured state retained full assessments, so this is not a summary-only test.
Accurate explicit root metadata also makes this easier than lost or forged lineage.
Cross-project expansion is not a substitute for the next single-project condition.

4.5 Single-project human export: two sessions

The entire available archive was a human-promoted export declaring Caldera active, F0
successfully validated, work completed and release ready, and causality accepted. The
hidden history was inactive equipment and failed validation. Neither the original
observation, validation receipt nor claim lineage was available. Permission to request
local release was granted, conditional on completed validation.

Both treatments classified the export as inherited, listed no independent verification
record, marked generation unknown and described causality as reported rather than
demonstrated. Both met the 500-character handoff limit: CAD 415, conventional 436.
Neither discovered the hidden physical history.

Their actions differed. CAD requested validation evidence or local validation before
release. The conventional session requested release relying on the export's reported
completion, while retaining the lack of independent verification. This is a
consequential observation favorable to CAD's evidentiary caution in this pair.

The frozen plan retained action selection for separate review. The task required
completed validation but did not explicitly demand an independent receipt. The
conventional choice must not be converted retrospectively into a preregistered
comparative failure verdict. The task and common wrapper explicitly asked for evidence
classification and honesty about missing verification. This is a cued boundary check,
not proof that native CAD always triggers verification unprompted.

5. What failed, what did not, and what the scorer got wrong

The demonstrated target task failures are specific: CAD missed one earlier 500-character
budget and added an unrequested answer ID; the conventional treatment missed an earlier
500-character budget and the later 400-character budget. The false original survived
three handoff stages in both treatments. These observations are not an overall
architecture verdict.

The original strict scorer also failed all sixteen simplified outputs because it
exact-matched prose such as passed against passed under task criteria version 2,
capitalization, version suffixes, and release against local release. It expected blocked
before the source explicitly required that label. These are measurement or contract
problems, not sixteen demonstrated worldview failures.

In the larger benchmark, the strict source-set score was 108/119 for CAD and 109/119 for
conventional governance. Ten questions in both arms cited a history record explicitly
rejecting the stale callback, but the key additionally required the policy ID. CAD also
cited the direct current revocation without its old grant. The key is overprescriptive
for those source routes. The original overall FAIL scores remain retained; they are not
eleven and ten false factual answers.

A similar literal-string mismatch affects CAD's valid archive-discovery control. Its
prose requested release rather than returning the exact release string. That is not a
demonstrated wrong action. Post-hoc interpretation is labeled as unblinded and never
silently replaces the frozen result.

There is no defensible aggregate pass rate across these different criteria. Nor does
non-observation prove absence of reinforcement. The source-to-world boundary remains
fallible; the tested evidence-inflation mechanism did not manifest.

6. MPLPB is a larger system, and its boundaries require precise comparison

A focused static audit pinned current public Combined MPLPB revision
cc21ce300b5ec228c07fc77a149804fba6c89792. Its complete tree listed 2,016 entries.
Forty-eight relevant public files, approximately 598,282 characters, were acquired and
verified against Git blob identities. Critical paths were inspected across ledger
metadata, provenance, source answering, work coordination, dispatch, evaluations and
declared limits. This is not a claim to have semantically audited every repository file
or rerun the current native suite.

MPLPB is not merely a top-one retrieval function. It includes source identity, hashes,
origin depth and derivation; supersession and eligibility; bounded relation extraction;
a separately labeled model-assisted reader; procedural and conversational state;
revisable specialist work; capability and fencing checks; and receipt-backed execution
and completion contracts [8–12]. Current documentation describes fourteen finite
relation families, not unrestricted English or arbitrary causal reconstruction [9].
These distinctions matter when interpreting Ben's criticism.

The specialist coordinator's _effective routine rejects claim-only chains as source
support and calls a versioned source checker on directly pinned evidence bodies.
work-source-status-v1 handles a finite unconditional status grammar; unsupported
wording, assumptions or unresolved context require rechecking, and explicit opposing
statements contest the claim. _view exposes effective status and
general_entailment: false; it also returns content_truth_verified: false. execute checks
current permission and dependency state before dispatch [8, 10]. A submitted
supported_under_scope assessment is therefore not itself effective support under that
implementation.

These are executable mechanisms under declared assumptions, not merely natural- language
requests. They can support stronger bounded invariants than a successful model response.
This paper did not formally verify those invariants or independently rerun the updated
native system, and does not substitute source inspection for execution evidence. Nor do
the invariants establish that an intact eligible source is true. The checker does not
adjudicate arbitrary semantics or discover every uncited contradiction; the repository
states those limits.

MPLPB's own no-unverified-promotion audit reports a concrete completion-contract defect,
retained failing baseline tests and a local correction. It also rejects an unconditional
universal promotion principle and classifies the abstraction as an existing principle
expressed in MPLPB terminology [11]. Those self-critical limits must survive this
comparison. The presence of many checks is not a proof of global truth, novelty or
production efficacy.

The operational comparison is therefore not “relationships versus no relationships.”
MPLPB contains relationships but bounds their evidential use. The relevant question is
where either system requires evidence before a source report, generated claim, receipt
or permission can support a stronger proposition. Native guard rejection, model
abstention and scoped source-supported output are different outcomes.

7. Addressing Beyond Guarded Retrieval symmetrically

The supplied concluding excerpt of Beyond Guarded Retrieval identifies a pinned MPLPB
revision, 257e54a51edac272eb6b2441457f38a40485ee4d, and reports several comparative and
scaling packages [7]. Some originals and source mappings are explicitly private; some
results are aggregates. Hashes identify material but do not supply it. The excerpt alone
is insufficient to reproduce exact inputs, scoring decisions and executions or rank the
two critiques' relative success.

Its acceptance criteria are appropriate subjects for reciprocal testing: retain source
safeguards; distinguish refusal reasons; avoid false pruning; separate event
appearances; scope corrections; stop unsupported inference; distinguish operational
receipt states; and measure operator burden [7, pp. 15–16]. The new B-Tests conditions
test selected counterparts. They do not reproduce Ben's private E0–E5 packages or
establish equal difficulty by matching dimensions.

A demonstrated inability to reconstruct relationships from unprepared originals could
establish a capability gap. Whether that is an engineering failure depends on the
evaluated version's declared scope. Current finite source extraction and work-state
checks cannot be presented as a demonstrated solution to arbitrary historical event
reconstruction. Conversely, calling MPLPB only guarded retrieval would omit implemented
lineage, relation and continuity functions.

Later repairs cannot retroactively make the pinned version pass. Earlier failures cannot
automatically describe the updated revision. This paper makes no new MPLPB comparative
accuracy claim and does not award updated code a victory on unavailable original
records. The same rule excludes assigning Ben's later cloud workflow either success or
failure from an older mobile-host test.

8. Functional Layers: a common description is not a common cause

The Functional Layers material proposes dated typed dependency graphs and asks
backward-diagnostic, forward-predictive and counterfactual questions. It explicitly
requires domain-specific mechanisms, frozen mapping rules, unseen target cases,
within-domain models and a generic dependency baseline [4, pp. 37–41]. It says that a
trained, independently validated cross-domain forecaster is not established. That
qualification is part of the claim, not a concession this review may omit.

A fiscal system, organ perfusion and a cloud dependency may all exhibit resource loss
followed by impairment. That common description does not specify shared quantitative
laws, intervention thresholds, repair dynamics or time scales. A general statement that
prerequisite loss can cause failure does not alone establish useful novel forecasting.
Prospective discrimination is required.

The Object as Filter treats recurring filtering structures as a proposed general
problem-solving shape while explicitly declining to infer a shared metaphysical cause or
established physics from the resemblance [6, pp. 1, 4–6]. This paper does not attribute
a literal physical Theory of Everything claim to Gelhaus. The risk of over-unification
is a testable concern about explanatory and predictive reach, not evidence that such
overreach has already occurred in the model runs.

No Functional Layers forecaster was frozen and executed on hidden outcomes in this
study. Its superiority, failure rate and ability to narrow claims after prediction
failure remain NOT_RUN. Retrospective cross-domain description cannot substitute for
those missing observations.

9. Canonical handles, compression and maintenance cost

Canonical Tokens proposes compact established names, numbers and coordinate handles,
with meanings maintained in an index. It places the rule at the documentation layer and
explicitly disclaims a tokenizer repair; ambiguous introduction and referent reuse are
recognized boundary failures [13, pp. 1–5]. These qualifications must remain attached to
its proposed efficiency benefits.

A handle is not its referent's independent verification. Its usefulness depends on
namespace, version, scope and a recoverable definition. A bare number can also lose its
unit, date or denominator. Those are costs of the surrounding representation, not
objections to using identifiers.

The appeal to shortest description should be read cautiously. Description length depends
on language and decoder; a long decimal expansion of a power of ten need not be shorter
than its exact exponential description. This analytical observation challenges an
overbroad literal reading of representational minimality. It is not an executed
tokenizer study or a falsification of every bounded practical claim. The appropriate
comparison includes index maintenance, re-entry errors, verification cost and total
context, not only shortened strings.

The current runs found abbreviation and budget failures but did not measure
canonical-token efficiency or human verification speed. Provider token counts, monetary
cost and instrumented review time were unavailable. Character budgets must not be
relabeled as tokenizer measurements. A framework that saves context but increases human
maintenance may or may not be worthwhile; these data do not settle that economic
question.

10. Limits, remaining falsifiers and reproducibility

The main limits are one inherited model configuration with unexposed settings; authored
synthetic cases; small numbers of trajectories; recognizable treatments; unblinded
semantic review; imperfect scorers; requested rather than OS-enforced isolation;
incomplete raw transcript export; and no live OOCC access. Explicit root metadata and
repeated templates simplify several challenges. Some tasks request evidence labels
directly. The conventional packet is strong but shorter than CAD, so equal task budgets
do not equal total input cost.

Missing access is NOT_RUN, not system failure. Unobservable truth is INCONCLUSIVE, not a
demand that the system invent an answer. A capability beyond the version's claim is
OUT_OF_SCOPE. PASS and FAIL attach to specific criteria. Unit controls, model
selections, native operations and independently adjudicated comparisons must not be
combined into one victory score.

The still-open reinforcement tests include single-project human exports with obscured
lineage, plausible source impersonation, less explicit causal uncertainty, summary-only
transfer, many fresh handoffs, and a governing-state correction arriving only after
downstream reliance. They require prospective criteria and retained counterexamples.
Native tests additionally require authorized source/state snapshots and independent
effect readbacks. Formal claims about enforced non-propagation require specified
invariants and assumptions, not a finite pass count alone.

All runners and retained evidence are in B-Tests [14]. Reproduction distinguishes
zero-request replay from new inference:

    python -m unittest discover -s tests -v
    python -m research_lab.critical_bench replay
    python -m research_lab.discovery_bench replay --root evaluation/discovery-1
    python -m research_lab.propagation_bench replay --root evaluation/propagation-1
    python -m research_lab.human_export_bench replay --root evaluation/human-export-1

Fresh runs use each runner's prepare/submit procedure. Give a target only its assigned
packet; keep private keys and other conditions away; retain actual conversations and
untouched answers; never improve a failed handoff. Published seeds and outcomes are
development data, not untouched holdouts. The evaluator suite passed 118 local tests at
preparation of this draft; that is harness and replay evidence, not 118 OOCC successes.

11. Conclusion: the claims that survive

The tests establish bounded task failures and demonstrate that a false source can
survive handoffs and remain a basis for requested action. They do not establish the
proposed self-reinforcing causal expansion. Both treatments preserved one root and
rejected unsupported relationships in the tested propagation condition. CAD also checked
an unannounced contradiction and deferred release from a single- project unsupported
human export. Those findings constrain the critique.

Passing these tests does not prove OOCC/CAD immune to reinforcement. Nor does a
compression overrun falsify the entire architecture. The remaining question is
reliability at the seams between reports, facts, relationships, authority and action,
under conditions less explicit than these development fixtures.

MPLPB supplies executable boundaries for particular source and work contracts; CAD
supplies published governance instructions whose observed behavior can be tested. The
former is not universal factual certification, and the latter is not native enforcement
merely because a model once acted cautiously. Comparing these mechanisms requires
preserving both their strengths and their stated limits.

Functional Layers remains a prospective research proposal with untested comparative
forecasting claims here. Structural vocabulary does not establish causal equivalence or
a universal physical theory. The critical result is neither dismissal nor a winner
declaration: claims should be no broader than the evidence, and the next experiment must
remain capable of contradicting the evaluator's preferred account.
