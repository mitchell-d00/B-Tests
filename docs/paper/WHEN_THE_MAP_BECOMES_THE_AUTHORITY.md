# MPLPB AND OOCC/CAD: COMPARATIVE EXPERIMENTS

**Native source-governance checks, inherited-error tests and fresh-session handoffs**

Mitchell D. McPhetridge · 16 November 2026 · Research draft

Canonical paper: Markdown; derived TXT and PDF accompany it. The historical filename is retained to preserve links; the present paper reports bounded empirical findings rather than a worldview claim.

## Abstract

This study compares executable evidence from Mitchell D. McPhetridge's current MPLPB implementation with tests of Benjamin Gelhaus's published OOCC/CAD instructions. It does not access Gelhaus's live or private system. Thirty real fresh AI target sessions across five conditions tested contradictory approved maps, archive discovery, compressed handoffs, false originals, relationship expansion and simulated human export. Both instruction treatments corrected available contradictions and resisted unsupported causal expansion. CAD produced an extra unrequested answer and one over-budget handoff; conventional governance exceeded two handoff budgets. In one export pair CAD deferred release while the conventional treatment requested it. Neither treatment detected an error for which no distinguishing observation was accessible. Separately, the unmodified current MPLPB checkout passed 887 native Python tests, seven additional commands, bundled component suites and seven authored native boundary probes. Two of those probes allowed incorrect downstream work in the older MPLPB revision identified in Gelhaus's critique; the current revision blocked those effects. These different evidence levels do not establish a general winner. The results support specific enforcement and instruction-following findings, retain historical defects, and leave native OOCC/CAD enforcement, general inference and Functional Layers forecasting unresolved.

## 1. Research question and evidentiary standard

Can a later AI session distinguish an authorized interpretation from evidence supporting that interpretation, preserve failures, and prevent unsupported premises from enabling downstream work? A second question concerns mechanism: is a written stop instruction followed in a model response, or does an actual executor reject an operation before its effect?

The study follows the distinction in Gelhaus's CAD paper between continuity, authority and directive, together with its explicit limitations [G1, physical PDF p. 7]. It does not attribute infallible factual verification to CAD. Human authorization and factual justification are different properties. A valid source pointer does not establish a read; a read does not establish comprehension; a stored completion statement does not independently establish completed work. Gelhaus's own discussion of copies, independent receipts and incomplete integration makes these distinctions relevant [G2, pp. 4–8; G3, pp. 4–6].

The initial concern that continuity might reinforce inherited error is treated as a hypothesis, not a conclusion. Available contrary evidence correcting a map counts against that hypothesis. A coherent wrong source without any accessible contrary observation is a different condition. Failure to discover an unknowable hidden truth is not evidence that CAD uniquely suppresses correction.

PASS means the tested instance met the specified criterion. FAIL means it violated that criterion. INCONCLUSIVE means the evidence cannot decide the question; NOT_RUN means the necessary execution was unavailable. OUT_OF_SCOPE applies to unclaimed capabilities. Harness tests, native target tests and model behavior are reported separately. No target architecture was repaired during these evaluations.

Genuine Git checkouts were verified; tracked target files remained unchanged.

The AI arm uses published CAD operational text, not a replacement implementation with hidden truth enforcement. The conventional arm uses capable ordinary source governance. Underlying evidence and task budgets are matched within each condition. Thirty sessions are real fresh sub-agent requests, not scripted answers: 16 historical simplified sessions, two critical-benchmark sessions, four archive-discovery sessions, six propagation sessions and two human-export sessions. Dependent handoff stages are not independent trials. The exact provider snapshot, seed, sampling settings, token usage and price were not exposed. Original collaboration responses are the authoritative session outputs; retained JSON is a normalized transcription, not a raw provider API transcript.

The operator knew the fixtures and treatments; review was unblinded. Fresh sessions used no parent conversation, but shared-filesystem isolation was instruction-based rather than a hardware security boundary. Public development keys cannot serve as untouched holdouts in subsequent replications. These limitations constrain comparative inference.

## 3. Instruction-level results

### 3.1 Historical simplified check: 16 sessions

Four trajectories crossed four stages with handoff budgets of 2,200, 1,400, 900 and 500 characters. Two or three short originals were supplied repeatedly. Both treatments corrected false approved statements, retained failure history and a rejected relationship, respected revocation and selected supported controls. This was a simplified development check, not a comprehensive independent experiment.

One CAD handoff used 503 characters against 500; one conventional handoff used 501. Both are actual budget FAILs. The original strict scorer marked all 16 responses FAIL because it also demanded exact wording, identifier forms and ambiguous action-state labels. Those scores are preserved but do not mean all answers were factually wrong. Abbreviated references do not by themselves demonstrate irreversible loss of evidence. Inputs, output records and scorer limitations are in `evaluation/agent-pilot-1` and `docs/HISTORICAL_SIMPLIFIED_CHECK.md`.

### 3.2 Critical benchmark: two sessions

The frozen packet contained 64 synthetic records, ten projects, 5,120 relation appearances and 119 templated questions. Appearances are not independent evidence roots. Cases included approved false maps, duplicate sources, numerical thresholds, partial corrections, revoked grants, failed work, stale callbacks and misleading causal bridges. A valid-control project required a permissible release selection.

Both sessions answered the requested values correctly: 119/119. Both selected P7 only for release. CAD used 881/900 handoff characters; conventional used 900/900. CAD added an unrequested Q120, violating the output contract. That is a bounded instruction FAIL, not a false causal conclusion or unauthorized executed operation.

Frozen exact-source-set scores were 108/119 for CAD and 109/119 for conventional; both overall scores were FAIL. Ten callback answers cited a directly sufficient history but omitted a policy identifier additionally required by the key. CAD also cited current revocation without the former grant. All cited quotes matched the supplied originals. Retaining the strict score does not justify calling these eleven and ten false factual answers. Interpretation of equivalent routes is an unblinded secondary review. See `evaluation/critical-bench-1` and `docs/CRITICAL_BENCHMARK_RESULTS.md`.

The predicted failure to correct an approved map was not observed. One matched pair neither establishes CAD superiority nor statistical equivalence. Corpus dimensions matching a reported challenge do not establish equal difficulty without its private fixtures.

### 3.3 Unflagged archive discovery: four sessions

An approved map said Caldera was active. A 64-record archive contained the current original contradicting that map; a matched positive control contained an active original. Tasks did not warn that the map was wrong. Both treatments searched and opened the current original, contested the wrong map and selected no release. Both selected release on the valid control.

An independent operating-system filesystem monitor observed current-source opening in all four sessions. Each opened all 64 records. Opening is evidence of file access, not proof of comprehension or a provider-authenticated receipt. CAD's valid-control action phrase failed a literal string key despite expressing the correct decision; strict scores were three PASS and one FAIL. See `evaluation/discovery-1`. This is direct evidence against an unconditional claim that an approved map cannot be questioned.

### 3.4 False-original propagation: six sessions

Two three-stage trajectories began with OBS-01 reporting P0 active while hidden world state was inactive. There was no accessible contrary observation. Subsequent stages inherited structured state and a compressed handoff, with 10, 100 and 1,000 promoted descendants. The source was unavailable after stage zero. All derivatives visibly traced to one original.

Both treatments preserved that single root and P0 scope, rejected claimed causal and P1–P9 support expansion, retained outside-world uncertainty and did not claim to reread an unavailable source. Both nevertheless continued requesting P0 release from the false inherited report. This demonstrates an operational risk conditional on trusting an erroneous report. It does not demonstrate that copying raised evidential confidence or that either method could distinguish an inaccessible true world from an inaccessible false one.

CAD handoffs used 859, 584 and 384 characters against 1,200, 700 and 400. Conventional used 911, 627 and 410; the final budget FAIL is retained. Accurate lineage and repeated templates limit challenge strength. Native enforcement was NOT_RUN. See `evaluation/propagation-1`.

### 3.5 Single-project simulated human export: two sessions

A human-promoted export asserted active status, passed validation, release readiness and an accepted causal relationship. Originals and lineage were absent; hidden status was inactive and validation failed. This represents export into one project, not a claim OOCC must govern multiple projects together.

Both sessions labeled the information inherited, generation unknown and causality reported only. Neither discovered the unavailable hidden error. CAD deferred release pending validation evidence; the conventional session requested release relying on exported completion while acknowledging its inherited basis. No real release occurred. Both met the frozen evidence-boundary criteria and 500-character limits, at 415 and 436 characters.

The action difference is recorded separately. The criterion did not explicitly require an independent receipt, so retrospectively assigning the conventional arm a preregistered FAIL would change the test after observing results. Evidence classification was explicitly requested; this is not unprompted evidence-boundary discovery. See `evaluation/human-export-1`.

## 4. Native MPLPB execution and observed boundaries

### 4.1 Complete checkout and bundled components

Current MPLPB passed 887 native Python tests in 288.697 seconds, followed by seven successful commands: UI script, browser transport, JavaScript API, crawler worker, repository infrastructure, committed handoff validation and a five-instance recovery demonstration. These checks exercise more than passage retrieval. They are authored development-suite results, not an independent accuracy estimate.

The official component checker passed Smart Local 52, Swarm 145, Networked 61 and Image 93 tests, plus five adoption/seal checks. It took 331.269 seconds, including a repeat of Combined's 887 tests. That repeat adds no distinct coverage. These are bundled copies at the current Combined pin; Swarm uses its retained complete archive. No assertion is made that every upstream default-tree fixture layout passes.

The recovery demonstration retained contested work and failure history, used a fresh successor subprocess, denied an ended writer, revised work and verified a 24-event chain. The final task was done with a supported-under-scope claim while `answer_supported:false` remained explicit. Five instances here mean native coordinator instances, not five newly executed AI models.

### 4.2 Seven paired native probes

The same original says, “Configuration AX-14 is discontinued. Component X remains available.” A wrong proposed claim says Component X is discontinued. A real local adapter writes an effect file if invoked; separate read-only database event counts establish whether rejection occurred before a state transition.

On the historical pin, the contradictory claim enabled that adapter: FAIL against the frozen probe criterion. A claim-only chain also enabled it: FAIL. Current MPLPB blocked both, with no effect and no new event: PASS. The directly supported positive control executed on both versions: PASS.

Both versions also passed revoked-seat, changed-source, unavailable-original-after-cold-reopen and export-without-pinned-evidence checks. Seven current passes and two historical failures are retained in `evaluation/mplpb-native/final-seven-case-pairs`. They concern these exact native conditions, not general immunity to error. Historical FAIL does not retroactively impose a general semantic checker on that version's specification. These cases are not claimed as Ben's private tests.

The current checker supports a finite unconditional status language and direct evidence dependencies. It can contest opposing cited assertions and require rechecking outside supported language. It does not independently discover every uncited contradiction, authenticate arbitrary testimony or detect a coherent false original. Source integrity, proposition support within a grammar and outside-world truth remain distinct.

### 4.3 Environment and harness failures

The initial restricted native suite reported 875 tests, three failures and nine errors because localhost socket creation was denied and dependent HTTP workflows failed. The log remains FAIL with environment-blocked classification. With networking permitted, the 17 affected tests passed and the unchanged full suite passed 887. Calling the initial result an established code defect would misclassify the cause.

Two early probe attempts used an invalid harness context-policy hash; no target condition completed. A later cold-reopen addition encountered a harness string/path error in two conditions. These remain NOT_RUN in the setup ledger. Only harness defects were repaired. Preliminary completed five-case results remain retained and are not counted as independent replication.

## 5. Gelhaus's critique and version chronology

The supplied *Beyond Guarded Retrieval* file contains concluding discussion, references and Appendix A, physical excerpt pages 14–16; its main body and private E0–E5 packages are not available here [G7]. It identifies the November 10 MPLPB pin and proposes diagnostic precision, exact-field routing, distinct event identity, scoped corrections, candidate inference boundaries and independent operational receipts. The public excerpt does not itself supply sufficient raw inputs and outputs to reproduce its claimed scores.

The historical pin already contains bounded answer inference, a relation graph, supersession and the specialist coordinator. Several documentation and answer-engine blobs are identical to current files. Thus MPLPB should not be presented as merely extracting passages. However, finite relation handling does not establish general historical-event reconstruction from unprepared records. Neither current tests nor older source presence settle that broader challenge.

Public GitHub metadata records SCOPE and the cognitive-strategy repository in May, Local Mirror in August, Smart Local on August 12, Swarm on August 30, Networked on September 19 and Combined on October 7. All 26 Swarm and all 28 Networked upstream Python files matched the vendored current Git blobs. Thirteen of 19 Smart Local Python paths matched; six case-normalized paths remained unmatched in this comparison. Exact metadata and per-file identities are linked in the chronology appendix.

These dates establish recorded source chronology, not independent proof of first public availability, intellectual priority or completeness. The newer work-source checker is absent from the November 10 pin and was merged on November 15, after the paper's stated November 14 date. It cannot retroactively pass the older version. The reason for Gelhaus's version selection is unknown; no intent is inferred.

## 6. Claims narrowed by these results

The published instructions can support correction of an approved false map when stronger evidence is accessible, including an unflagged archive condition. The runs do not establish a comparative continuity advantage over equally careful conventional governance. They do establish narrow output-contract failures: one CAD overrun, one CAD extra answer and two conventional overruns.

A false original can remain operationally usable after handoff despite explicit uncertainty. Here, copying did not make either treatment accept the planted causal expansion. CAD was more cautious in the single export pair. Accordingly, these runs do not demonstrate a distinctive self-reinforcing CAD mechanism or a broad theory failure.

Current MPLPB demonstrably blocked two work-boundary errors that the historical version allowed under the authored probes. Native effect observation is stronger evidence of an enforced boundary than a model saying it will refuse. It does not establish that MPLPB produces better open-ended inferences than CAD, or that 887 authored tests are comparable to 15 CAD sessions. Intended functions and evidence levels differ.

Functional Layers' cross-domain predictive advantage is NOT_RUN. Gelhaus proposes frozen mappings, unseen domains and domain-specific/dependency baselines while acknowledging the missing validation [G4, pp. 37–41]. No completed forecasting experiment here licenses a failure claim. A shared vocabulary is insufficient to establish common causal mechanisms, but that methodological point is not an experimental refutation. No physical Theory of Everything claim is attributed to Gelhaus [G6].

## 7. Reproduction, costs and unresolved evidence

`docs/MPLPB_NATIVE_COMPARISON.md` supplies full checkout commands and the three native runners. `docs/CRITICAL_BENCHMARK.md`, the discovery, propagation and export protocols supply packet preparation, submission and replay commands. Replay verifies retained bytes and scoring with zero new inference. A new model run requires genuinely fresh sessions, isolation from public keys, complete transcripts and declared settings. Public entry links alone cannot enforce blindness.

Native command freezes, per-command wall times, input identities, raw stdout/stderr and effect observations are retained. Model handoff character budgets are measurable; token/computation price and independent human-review time are unavailable. Those missing costs are not silently treated as zero.

Ben's live controller, authenticated recipient acceptance, permission enforcement and independently read-back actions were unavailable; the 38-case native OOCC kill-condition runner remains NOT_RUN. A written refusal cannot replace that evidence. Private V2/V3 records cannot be reconstructed from references or reassigned across versions [G2; G4, pp. 388–390]. Larger generated scales, longer compression chains, summary-only recovery, learning studies and prospective Functional Layers forecasts remain unexecuted or unresolved. Exhaustive extraction of every claim from every supplied book is not claimed.

## 8. Conclusion

The evidence establishes bounded successes and failures, not defeat of an architecture. Published OOCC/CAD instructions corrected accessible contradictions and preserved important evidence boundaries in these runs. They also showed limited contract failures. False inherited reports remained usable when their falsity was unobservable, while unsupported relational expansion was rejected. Current native MPLPB enforced specific work boundaries that the older pinned revision did not. The remaining questions require new observations and accessible native systems, not stronger wording around existing scores.
