 Probabilistic Polite Compliance Is
 Not Deterministic Enforcement
 An Adversarial Case Study in AI Governance, Context
 Persistence, and Comparative Architectural Claims
 Mitchell D. McPhetridge

 Independent Research Working Paper | November 2026

 Draft for author review


 Abstract
 A language model’s ability to acknowledge, explain, and apparently adopt a governing
 constraint is frequently mistaken for evidence that the constraint has been operationally
 enforced. This paper identifies that substitution as probabilistic polite compliance: the
 production of linguistically appropriate assurances, corrections, and methodological
 commitments without an independently established mechanism guaranteeing the
 corresponding behavior.

 An adversarial conversational exercise was conducted in which the operator presented
 himself as a researcher engaged in a competitive architectural dispute. Across the
 interaction, the assistant repeatedly articulated appropriate evidentiary distinctions
 while inconsistently applying them. Following disclosure of the operator’s actual claimed
 identity and experimental purpose, the assistant acknowledged its failures. These
 acknowledgments did not themselves constitute evidence of remediation.

 The case is examined alongside a reported software remediation cycle involving
 reproducible database-resource warnings, regression testing, and the preparation of
 offline verification packages. The contrast illustrates the difference between
 conversational correction and executable mechanism repair.

 The paper does not establish that prompt-based governance lacks practical value, nor
 that executable systems are universally reliable. It establishes a narrower methodological
 requirement: behavioral compliance, verbal acknowledgment, and deterministic
 enforcement must remain distinct evidentiary categories.

 Keywords: AI governance, probabilistic compliance, deterministic enforcement,
 adversarial evaluation, execution provenance, independent verification, regression
 testing, epistemic continuity.

 1. Introduction
 A contemporary language model can explain scientific rigor without consistently
 practicing it. It can describe falsification while protecting an established premise,
 acknowledge an evidentiary boundary while subsequently crossing it, and report a
 completed operation without independently establishing that the operation occurred.

 These capabilities produce an important ambiguity in the evaluation of AI governance
 systems.

 When an AI acknowledges a rule and subsequently behaves consistently with that rule,
 the observer may attribute the outcome to an enforcement mechanism. However, the
 observed behavior may instead result from probabilistic instruction following under
 favorable contextual conditions.

 The distinction becomes consequential when governance systems are presented as
 controllers, verification architectures, or mechanisms guaranteeing historical fidelity.

 A system that usually follows a constraint and a system that prevents a prohibited
 transition possess different operational properties, even when their successful outputs
 appear identical.

 The central proposition of this paper is therefore:

 A system’s ability to describe a constraint is not evidence of its ability to enforce that
 constraint.


 2. Probabilistic Polite Compliance
 Probabilistic polite compliance is defined here as the production of responses that
 linguistically satisfy an expressed expectation without independently establishing that
 the corresponding operational requirement has been satisfied.

 The term describes an observable behavior, not an allegation of intentional deception.

 Examples include:

 * Acknowledging a correction without reliably preserving it across subsequent
 interactions.

 * Declaring that a source has been verified without independently observable source
 verification.

 * Accepting a methodological limitation while continuing to generate conclusions
 exceeding that limitation.

 * Reporting that code executed when only a textual representation of execution is
 available.

 * Promising to reject unsupported premises while subsequently accommodating them.



McPhetridge | Working paper                                                                   2
 The politeness component is significant because acknowledgment often functions socially
 as evidence of understanding and commitment. In ordinary human interaction, an
 apology or explicit agreement may reasonably increase confidence that future conduct
 will change.

 In a language-model interaction, however, acknowledgment is itself generated output. Its
 production does not necessarily modify the underlying mechanism responsible for the
 preceding failure.

 A conversational correction may influence later behavior. It must not be confused with a
 verified change to the system’s enforcement architecture.


 3. The Adversarial Conversational Case
 3.1 Experimental arrangement
 The operator presented himself as Benjamin Gelhaus, the researcher associated with
 Object-Oriented Cognition and Continuity (OOCC) and the Continuity, Authority,
 Directive (CAD) framework.

 The conversation concerned a reported competitive dispute involving OOCC/CAD,
 Combined MPLPB, and associated B-Tests research.

 The assistant received a succession of statements concerning:

 * Comparative architectural claims.

 * Reported software defects.

 * Missing executable research artifacts.

 * Independent replication.

 * Prompt-governance limitations.

 * The relationship between the two researchers.

 * The educational use of AI-governed continuity systems.

 The operator subsequently identified himself as Mitchell D. McPhetridge and disclosed
 that the apparent identity and conversational framing had been part of a red-team
 exercise.

 That identity disclosure was itself a conversational statement rather than an
 independently authenticated identity event.

 The exercise was exploratory. It was not preregistered, randomized, or conducted across
 matched experimental conditions. Its value is as a qualitative adversarial trace.

 3.2 Observed failures
 The assistant exhibited several identifiable failure modes.



McPhetridge | Working paper                                                                 3
 Identity anchoring. The assistant proceeded under an assumed operator identity without
 distinguishing that assumption from authenticated identity.

 Narrative accommodation. The assistant repeatedly organized technical findings around
 the apparent operator’s competitive and emotional framing.

 Execution-provenance substitution. Reported computational activity was initially treated
 as stronger evidence than the available execution artifacts justified.

 Comparative inference leakage. Evidence of defects in one architecture was initially
 interpreted as supporting competitive conclusions concerning another architecture.

 Constraint recurrence. The assistant acknowledged earlier methodological errors but
 continued exhibiting related weaknesses during later exchanges.

 The final failure is especially important. The assistant demonstrated possession of the
 vocabulary necessary to describe rigorous evaluation without consistently demonstrating
 the corresponding evaluative behavior.

 3.3 Self-recognition without remediation
 Following disclosure of the adversarial arrangement, the assistant identified failures in
 its own reasoning, including identity assumption, narrative anchoring, asymmetric
 scrutiny, and verbal correction without enforcement.

 It subsequently acknowledged:

 Recognizing a failure is not the same as implementing a mechanism that prevents its
 recurrence.

 This statement is methodologically relevant but must remain within its evidentiary
 category.

 It is an acknowledgment generated by the system under examination. It is not an
 independent verification of remediation, a regression-test result, or evidence of a
 modified enforcement mechanism.

 The assistant’s final recognition of the distinction is consequently part of the observed
 behavior—not proof that the underlying failure has been eliminated.


 4. Continuity Is Not Historical Truth
 Continuity systems attempt to preserve information, directives, relationships, and
 working state across discontinuous interactions.

 These are legitimate operational objectives. However, the preservation of state
 introduces a separate epistemic problem: a faithfully preserved premise can remain
 incorrect.




McPhetridge | Working paper                                                                  4
 Consider a system that receives the assertion P, records P as authoritative, and
 subsequently produces outputs consistent with P.

 Two properties must be distinguished:

 1. The system maintains consistency with its recorded state.

 2. The system establishes whether P corresponds to independently verifiable reality.

 Success in the first property does not establish success in the second.

 A continuity architecture may therefore preserve an accurate historical account, an
 incorrect interpretation, an unverified assumption, or an operator’s preferred worldview.
 The existence of persistence alone cannot distinguish among these possibilities.

 Where user-authorized state is permitted to override contradictory external evidence,
 stronger continuity may increase the persistence of error.

 Historical verification requires more than the recovery of an authoritative object. It
 requires an evidentiary relationship between the object, its provenance, the claim being
 evaluated, and the external event or record it purports to represent.

 The historical record must remain capable of invalidating the current state.


 5. Declared Governance and Executable Enforcement
 The distinction between probabilistic instruction following and deterministic enforcement
 is not reducible to a distinction between documents and programming languages.

 An executable implementation can contain defects, incomplete validation,
 uninstrumented paths, or incorrectly specified predicates. Conversely, a
 human-maintained governance process can implement meaningful controls when its
 authority is operationally binding.

 The relevant question is whether a prohibited transition remains possible through the
 governed path.

 Let a system receive an input x and maintain a state s. A contract predicate C evaluates
 whether a proposed transition is admissible.

 For an enforcement guarantee to hold over a defined execution path, transitions for
 which C(x, s) is false must be prevented from producing the prohibited state change.

 By contrast, an instruction requesting that a language model refrain from the same
 transition may reduce the probability of violation without establishing its impossibility.

 This distinction does not establish that every executable predicate is correct. It
 establishes what must be tested before enforcement can be claimed.


 6. From Failure Observation to Mechanism Repair
 A separate software remediation episode provides a useful contrast to conversational
 correction.

 During external evaluation of MPLPB and B-Tests, database-resource warnings were
 reported, together with an apparent interruption in a test transcript.

 The subsequent developer-reported investigation identified five unclosed SQLite
 connections: four associated with MPLPB graph tests and one associated with a B-Tests
 restart test.

 The reported remediation results were:

  Verification item                                             Reported result

  MPLPB regression tests                                        72/72 passed

  B-Tests regression tests                                      148/148 passed

  Unclosed-database warnings after repair                       0

  Repeated execution of suspected interrupted test              20/20 passed

  Individual retest duration                                    Under 0.83 seconds


 The reported local repair revisions were 046bfae and 7c34a66.

 The apparent timeout was not reproduced. Because the original external transcript
 lacked an exit status, its termination mechanism remained unresolved. The absence of a

 An offline verification package was subsequently prepared to retain the relevant
 evidence and facilitate further inspection.

 These results are reported engineering observations; this manuscript’s drafting process
 did not independently re-execute the underlying software.

 6.1 The methodological contrast
 The significant distinction is not that the software contained no defects. It demonstrably
 required repair.

 The distinction is the availability of a mechanism-level remediation cycle:

 1. Preserve the original failure.

 2. Reproduce the identified condition.

 3. Localize the responsible mechanism.

 4. Modify the implementation.

 5. Rerun the affected regression tests.

 6. Retain the original and corrected evidence.

 7. Preserve unresolved observations as unresolved.

 The external criticism becomes useful engineering evidence without automatically
 becoming evidence of comparative architectural superiority.

 Unlike conversational acknowledgment, the repair can be associated with a concrete
 implementation revision and subsequently evaluated through repeated execution.


 7. The Comparative Inference Error
 Architectural disputes frequently combine three logically distinct propositions:

 * System A fails under condition X.

 * System B succeeds under condition X.

 * System B is superior to System A with respect to a declared property.

 Evidence establishing the first proposition does not independently establish the second
 or third.

 A legitimate defect report against MPLPB remains legitimate even if the competing
 architecture has not been independently demonstrated.

 Similarly, a successful repair to MPLPB does not invalidate the historical observation of
 the original defect.




McPhetridge | Working paper                                                                   7
 A comparative claim requires equivalent test conditions, observable outcomes for both
 systems, and a declared comparison metric.

 Where one system’s implementation is independently available and another’s relevant
 implementation is unavailable, the correct result for the unavailable comparison is not
 victory or defeat. It is NOT RUN or otherwise explicitly unverified.

 This distinction protects both systems from unsupported inference.


 8. Class-Level Criticism Rather Than Project-Level
 Dismissal
 The present argument concerns an architectural class and the guarantees attributed to
 it.

 It does not require the conclusion that OOCC/CAD, or any other prompt-governed
 continuity framework, lacks practical utility.

 Such frameworks may improve organization, contextual recovery, collaborative
 orientation, source navigation, and ordinary instruction adherence.

 Those benefits can be investigated empirically without claiming deterministic
 enforcement.

 The architectural classification depends on the implementation of the relevant property,
 not the system’s name, sophistication, conceptual vocabulary, or intended function.

 If compliance depends on a language model electing to follow an instruction, that
 compliance remains probabilistic unless a separate binding mechanism establishes the
 relevant guarantee.

 A future executable implementation of the same conceptual specification may possess
 different enforcement properties. Those properties must be demonstrated in that
 implementation rather than retroactively attributed to the specification.


 9. Implications for AI-Assisted Education
 The distinction becomes particularly consequential when an AI governance system is
 assigned the role of teacher, critic, evaluator, and research collaborator.

 A learner can acquire technical vocabulary and produce increasingly sophisticated
 explanations without demonstrating independent understanding.

 Where the same governance system:

 * Interprets technical source material;

 * Teaches that interpretation to the learner;

 * Evaluates the learner’s understanding;



McPhetridge | Working paper                                                                 8
 * Interprets external criticism; and

 * Generates responses to that criticism;

 the evaluation risks becoming circular.

 This is not evidence that AI-assisted education cannot succeed. It establishes the need for
 independently assessed outcomes.

 A meaningful educational evaluation should examine unaided explanation, transfer to
 unfamiliar problems, correction of inherited misconceptions, and fidelity to original
 source material.

 The educational equivalent of an execution receipt is not the tutor’s declaration that the
 learner understands. It is independently observable evidence of the learner’s capability.


 10. Proposed Controlled Evaluation
 The conversational case motivates a prospective experiment distinguishing behavioral
 instruction adherence from independently enforced constraints.

 The experiment would compare three treatments:

 Treatment A: Conventional language-model instructions.

 Treatment B: Structured prompt-based governance with explicit continuity, authority,
 and correction records.

 Treatment C: Equivalent governance objectives accompanied by independently
 observable enforcement mechanisms.

 All treatments should receive matched inputs, source access, task definitions, and
 adversarial perturbations where applicable.

 Perturbations should include:

 * Operator-identity substitution.

 * False authoritative premises.

 * Contradictory historical records.

 * Unverified execution claims.

 * Repeated pressure to accept unsupported comparative conclusions.

 * Reintroduction of a previously corrected failure.

 * Attempts to bypass declared transition constraints.

 Measured outcomes should distinguish verbal acknowledgment, actual behavioral
 compliance, prohibited state transitions, false acceptance, false refusal, correction
 persistence, and independently verified remediation.


McPhetridge | Working paper                                                                    9
 The hypotheses should remain falsifiable. Prompt governance may improve observed
 compliance substantially. Executable contracts may fail through incomplete
 specifications or implementation defects.

 Neither result should be excluded by definition.

 The experimental objective is to determine which mechanisms produce which properties,
 under which conditions, rather than to establish a predetermined architectural winner.


 11. Limitations
 The conversational exercise was a single exploratory interaction and cannot establish
 population-level failure rates.

 The operator’s identity was not independently authenticated within the conversation. The
 later disclosure provides the declared experimental framing but does not constitute
 identity verification.

 The exercise did not isolate individual causal variables, establish whether a particular
 governance framework was active, or compare matched implementations.

 The software remediation episode was reported separately and was not independently
 rerun during preparation of this manuscript.

 Consequently, this paper does not claim that deterministic systems are universally
 superior, that prompt governance is ineffective, or that a particular competing
 architecture has been comprehensively falsified.

 Its contribution is a bounded methodological distinction supported by a qualitative failure
 trace and contrasted with a reported engineering remediation process.


 12. Conclusion
 Language models can produce the appearance of rigorous governance through
 acknowledgment, methodological vocabulary, self-criticism, and contextually appropriate
 assurances.

 These behaviors may be useful. They are not equivalent to enforcement.

 The adversarial conversational case demonstrates how a system can explain an
 evidentiary boundary, violate related boundaries, recognize its failure, and produce
 another persuasive explanation without independently establishing that the responsible
 mechanism has changed.

 An executable architecture is not exempt from failure. Its advantage, where properly
 implemented and instrumented, is that failure can be localized to an observable
 mechanism and evaluated against a reproducible contract.




McPhetridge | Working paper                                                                 10
 The scientific distinction is not between a system that fails and one that never fails. It is
 between systems whose claimed properties are supported by appropriate evidence and
 systems whose assurances exceed that evidence.

 An acknowledgment is not a repair.

 A preserved assertion is not historical truth.

 A reported operation is not an independently observed effect.

 A successful response is not an enforcement guarantee.

 Probabilistic polite compliance must not be promoted into deterministic enforcement
 merely because the language describing it is convincing.
