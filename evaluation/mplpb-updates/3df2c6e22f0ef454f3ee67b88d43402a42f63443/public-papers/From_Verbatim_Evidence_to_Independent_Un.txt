HEADMASTER 
From Verbatim Evidence to Independent Understanding 
A Function-First, Source-Grounded AI Teaching System 
 
Benjamin Gelhaus 
Object-Oriented Cognition and Continuity (OOCC) / Voltron 
Research manuscript • November 2026 
Research and editorial assistance: ChatGPT (GPT-6) 
 
ABSTRACT 
Headmaster is a partially functioning Android learning application that extends Object-Oriented 
Cognition and Continuity (OOCC) into adaptive, source-grounded instruction. Its design grew 
from a practical problem of authorship and evidence. Gelhaus reports that a judge approved AI 
as an ADA accommodation for use in the courtroom with counsel but restricted its use during 
testimony. The resulting challenge—how to organize and communicate a person’s words 
without replacing them—led to the DHS Translation Protocol, which preserves original 
language, context, chronology, and identifiable edits. The same principle then suggested an 
educational application: learning assistance should help a student develop an understanding 
while preserving the student’s actual answers, uncertainties, and revisions. Headmaster 
combines this authorship principle with Gelhaus’s self-directed method of exploring functions, 
mechanisms, relationships, and constraints. The first proposed demanding test is Mitchell D. 
McPhetridge’s technical writing. This paper describes the conceptual lineage, system design, 
existing prototype evidence, and an evaluation protocol that asks whether learners can explain 
and apply unfamiliar material independently after tutoring.  
 
Keywords: intelligent tutoring systems; source fidelity; authorship; functional mapping; learner 
modeling; adaptive instruction; self-explanation; AI-assisted learning; educational assessment. 
 
1. INTRODUCTION 
A useful learning system must do more than present a convincing explanation. It must help a 
person acquire an understanding that belongs to that person—one that can be expressed, 
defended, tested, and applied without borrowing the tutor’s language. 
 
Headmaster approaches that problem through two connected commitments. First, the original 
record matters: a source author’s statements, a learner’s answers, and an AI tutor’s 
interpretations must remain distinguishable. Second, instruction must be functional: learning a 
term is only a starting point; competence requires understanding what a system or claim does, 
why it works, which conditions it requires, and where it fails. 
 
The application is under active development as part of the OOCC/Voltron ecosystem. Gelhaus 
reports that substantial portions of the Android prototype work on his device. Versioned source 
and engineering records document a functioning interface and several implemented 
learning-state features, while the full adaptive instructional loop remains a subject of 
development and evaluation [P3–P6]. 
 
The research question is whether a source-faithful, function-first tutor can help a learner 
understand difficult technical work more deeply than conventional reading assistance or a 
generic AI explanation. 
 
2. DEVELOPMENT: FROM DHS TO HEADMASTER 
The initiating problem was practical rather than academic. Gelhaus describes receiving court 
approval to use AI as an ADA accommodation while present with his lawyer, but not while he 
was on the witness stand. The judge explained the restriction, and Gelhaus began treating the 
explanation as a set of design constraints. The essential concern was whether AI-assisted 
organization or presentation would preserve his own language and meaning. 
 
His response was the DHS Translation Protocol. It begins with the speaker’s exact words and 
protects their original context. Readability can be improved by arranging intact passages, 
identifying topics, and supplying navigation. When new explanatory language is required, that 
language belongs to a visibly separate AI-authored layer. Corrections, changes, source order, 
speaker identity, and provenance remain accessible [P1]. The related application blueprint 
expands this into an operational design for speech, writing, evidence, and reader-facing 
representations [P2]. 
 
Once this method existed, its wider usefulness became apparent. Schoolwork presented a 
closely related problem: if an AI writes a polished explanation for a student, the result can 
conceal what that student actually understands. A learner needs assistance without losing 
authorship of the answers and reasoning that demonstrate progress. 
 
The next step followed naturally from Gelhaus’s own experience teaching himself unfamiliar 
subjects through conversation, source retrieval, functional comparisons, revisions, and 
experiments. What if this process could be made reproducible in software? Headmaster 
emerged as the educational branch: retain the original person’s voice, discover what the person 
understands, find what is missing, and actually teach the next step. 
 
The transfer from court-related communication to education is the project’s defining design 
lineage. The court account describes the problem that motivated the system; the educational 
research examines what the resulting method can accomplish. The exact judicial wording can 
be cited from the record if it becomes part of a later legal or historical edition. 
 
3. RESEARCH PROPOSITION 
Headmaster’s central proposition is that source-grounded function mapping, paired with 
interactive teaching and learner-authored assessment, can improve the acquisition of difficult 
concepts. 
 
A conventional explanation often begins with definitions and a polished synopsis. A function-first 
lesson begins by asking: What is the object or claim? What does it do? What inputs or premises 
does it require? What mechanism connects them to an outcome? What are the limits, competing 
interpretations, and counterexamples? 
 
This approach is particularly suited to interdisciplinary work, where familiar words may carry 
unfamiliar technical meanings. It can reveal hidden prerequisites while also protecting against 
false analogy: two systems may share a function without sharing a mechanism. 
 
The testable hypothesis is that, after comparable study time, learners using source-anchored 
function-first tutoring will perform better on unaided explanation and novel application than 
learners using ordinary reference resources or a standard AI explanatory assistant. A 
complementary hypothesis is that keeping the learner’s exact answers and corrections will 
improve the accuracy of mastery assessment and continuity between teaching sessions. 
 
These claims are evaluated through performance, not through the apparent fluency of the tutor. 
 
4. PEDAGOGICAL FOUNDATION 
Headmaster draws from several established lines of educational research. Concept and 
knowledge mapping provides ways to organize explicit relationships among ideas [R1]. The 
ICAP framework links the form of cognitive engagement with learning processes, giving a 
rationale for interactive questioning rather than passive exposure [R2]. Research on prompted 
self-explanation supports asking students to construct their own accounts of mechanisms and 
ideas [R3]. Retrieval-practice findings motivate later testing without access to the original 
explanation [R4]. Knowledge-tracing research provides an established precedent for estimating 
changing student competence from observed work [R5]. 
 
Headmaster combines these ideas with OOCC’s emphasis on protected source text, correction 
history, functional relationships, and recoverable state. Its proposed contribution lies in how 
these elements interact within an actual application, rather than in claiming to invent the 
individual educational techniques. 
 
The distinction between *having encountered* an explanation and *being able to reproduce and 
use* the underlying idea is fundamental. Headmaster therefore stores exposure, vocabulary 
recognition, worked examples, independent explanation, and demonstrated transfer as different 
forms of evidence. 
 
5. FUNCTION-FIRST SOURCE MODEL 
The source mapper turns each studied claim into a connected instructional object. Its core fields 
include: 
 
• Source identity: author, document, revision, passage, page or section, and access rights. 
• Concept or object: the entity, claim, process, or system being studied. 
• Function: its purpose, behavior, or predicted result. 
• Inputs and outputs: required premises, information, materials, states, and expected 
consequences. 
• Mechanism: the logical or causal explanation of how the function operates. 
• Dependencies: prerequisite concepts and skills. 
• Constraints: conditions under which the claim applies and boundaries on its use. 
• Counterexamples: cases that challenge the explanation or expose its limits. 
• Relationships: typed links such as DEPENDS_ON, REFINES, CONTRADICTS, and 
RELATED_TO. 
• Attribution and evidence: what the original author states, what the tutor infers, and what the 
learner independently proposes. 
• Revision history: the original record together with later questions, corrections, and decisions. 
 
A function map is an instructional representation, not a substitute for the original paper. The 
source remains available for checking formal notation, qualifications, and context. Connections 
that merely resemble one another remain labeled as analogies until documentary or 
experimental evidence supports a stronger relationship. 
 
This object model also provides a practical bridge to the OOCC functional-layers research, 
which studies recurring functions across distinct domains without treating those domains as 
identical [P7]. 
 
6. THE HEADMASTER LEARNING CYCLE 
The application’s intended instructional cycle has six connected stages [P3]. 
 
1. Source intake. A student chooses a subject or imports authorized material: PDFs, textbooks, 
notes, code, written work, video transcripts, or existing research. The system keeps the original 
document and reports exactly what was extracted. Local extraction and economical batching 
can reduce repeated paid AI calls. 
 
2. Concept reconstruction. Headmaster identifies chapters, sections, concepts, functions, 
mechanisms, dependencies, and disagreements. The relationships are linked back to passages 
and organized into a navigable map. 
 
3. Learner diagnosis. The tutor examines the learner’s own answers, not just prior exposure. It 
distinguishes recognized terminology from explanatory competence, uncertain understanding, 
missing prerequisites, and independent application. Source-authored, AI-authored, 
learner-authored, and unattributed text are kept separate. 
 
4. Lesson selection. The system chooses a specific instructional frontier: a missing prerequisite, 
a misunderstood mechanism, an overlooked constraint, or a promising connection to knowledge 
the learner already possesses. 
 
5. Conversational teaching. Headmaster acts as an instructor. It explains, demonstrates, asks 
questions, responds to confusion, proposes examples, and revises its approach. Books and 
videos remain instructional resources; the teaching interaction stays in Headmaster’s own chat. 
 
6. Independent assessment and continuity. The learner explains the material in original words, 
predicts outcomes under new conditions, and confronts counterexamples. Results and 
corrections become part of the learner’s continuing history. A later tutor can recover the 
authorized record and resume without pretending to be the same AI instance. 
 
A complete lesson therefore ends with evidence of the learner’s capability, not simply with a 
generated answer. 
 
7. LEARNER AUTHORSHIP AND CONTINUITY 
Headmaster’s learner record is designed to be both educational and evidentiary. It preserves 
the student’s exact statement, the question or task that prompted it, the source being studied, 
the tutor’s response, and later corrections. AI-produced prose can be useful instructional 
material, but it is stored under its own authorship. 
 
This separation solves a practical problem in assessment. An eloquent answer copied from a 
model is weak evidence of personal understanding. A tentative answer, followed by targeted 
instruction and a later independently generated solution, is much more informative. Errors are 
part of the record because they help locate the lesson that should come next. 
 
The application’s broader OOCC design also treats the curriculum, tutor, specialists, learner 
profile, and source maps as individually addressable components. Headmaster operates within 
the main Voltron environment while retaining its own chat and learning state. The Round Table 
design allows specialists to examine different aspects of a learning task under a scheduler, with 
the human learner participating rather than being replaced. Existing three-worker prototypes 
and the later four-specialist target are separate implementation stages [P4–P6]. 
 
Persistent communication and research records allow successors to continue the work while 
preserving distinct writer identities. For instruction, this means a new tutor should know which 
concepts were taught, what the learner actually demonstrated, which misconceptions remain, 
and why the preceding lesson stopped. 
 
8. McPHETRIDGE AS THE FIRST 
HIGH-DIFFICULTY TEST 
Mitchell D. McPhetridge’s work provides a compelling first-person challenge for Headmaster. 
Gelhaus has described difficulty understanding McPhetridge’s technical writing despite wanting 
to engage with it seriously. The goal is to develop enough independent comprehension to 
assess the work on its own terms, including its mechanisms, assumptions, and possible 
weaknesses. 
 
The preserved Gelhaus–McPhetridge correspondence records sustained dialogue about 
constraints, recursive systems, verification, and credit for borrowed ideas [P8]. That exchange 
establishes the context for this study. McPhetridge’s original publications, rather than summaries 
of the correspondence, will supply the actual teaching passages. His authorship and 
terminology remain intact throughout the experiment. 
 
The pilot proceeds as follows. First, select an exact paper or chapter and record its version, 
source, and learning objectives. Second, ask Gelhaus to explain its main claim unaided, 
identifying what he understands and what remains opaque. Third, have Headmaster construct a 
bounded function map and teach the difficult passages through dialogue. Fourth, remove the 
teaching support and ask Gelhaus to reconstruct the argument, explain its mechanism, identify 
the conditions under which it holds, and evaluate a new case. Fifth, repeat the assessment after 
a delay and compare with the initial baseline. 
 
A meaningful success would be visible in the learner’s own work: accurate explanation, original 
examples, recognition of limitations, and the ability to distinguish McPhetridge’s argument from 
Gelhaus’s subsequent interpretation. This pilot can establish feasibility and generate detailed 
evidence; broader educational claims require additional learners and comparisons. 
 
9. CURRENT APPLICATION AND ENGINEERING 
STATUS 
Headmaster is already represented by a working Android prototype and a substantial set of 
engineering artifacts. Gelhaus reports that significant parts of the program function on his 
phone, while the application remains under active development. Screenshots reviewed in the 
engineering work show an installed v247 interface with local chat behavior and 
model-assignment controls [P5–P6]. The exact version currently installed on the owner’s device 
has not yet been established from a fresh device readout. 
 
The development records describe implemented local learner-state structures, original-quotation 
retrieval, source-linked learning-path annotations, tutor setup, and a growing functional mapping 
layer. The v247 release was followed by an APK-signature repair; the v248 engineering notes 
report a model-catalog setup change and 74 passing host assertions [P5–P6]. The dependency 
audit also identifies unresolved integration work across native worker identity, source 
consumption, Android acceptance, and controller-issued permissions [P4]. 
 
The most consequential unfinished work is the complete teaching loop: robust multi-source 
intake, genuine model-backed conversational instruction, visible specialist coordination, 
independently assessed learner progress, and reliable continuation after a restart. Earlier 
diagnostics also report scanning and import interruptions. These are specific engineering 
targets within a real application, rather than placeholders for a program that has never been 
built. 
 
The next development audit should establish a current on-device version and a 
feature-by-feature functional inventory, preserving the distinction between working Android 
behavior, host-tested source code, and planned capabilities. 
 
10. EXPERIMENTAL DESIGN 
The initial research phase is a documented single-learner pilot using one technically demanding 
original text. Each claim taught must have a stable source anchor. Before instruction, the learner 
completes a baseline explanation and predicts how a central mechanism would behave in a 
new example. Headmaster then teaches the material with a function map, guided dialogue, and 
exercises. After instruction, an evaluator scores an unaided explanation and a novel application. 
A delayed assessment measures retention. 
 
The proposed rubric assesses five competencies, each scored from 0 to 4: source fidelity; 
mechanism explanation; correct premises and constraints; counterexample or transfer 
reasoning; and calibrated attribution and uncertainty. The 20-point total is interpreted together 
with the original answer record, not as an unexplained mastery label. A scorer who can read the 
original technical source should apply a rubric defined before the lesson. 
 
A second phase can compare three conditions with consenting learners and balanced study 
materials: ordinary source reading with reference aids; conventional AI explanation; and 
Headmaster’s source-linked, function-first tutoring. Study time and available materials should be 
comparable. Passage assignment and condition order should be counterbalanced, with blinded 
scoring where feasible. 
 
Primary outcomes are unaided explanation accuracy and transfer to a new problem. Secondary 
outcomes include retention, time to an accurate account, identification of assumptions, 
correction of misconceptions, appropriate confidence, tutoring cost, and successful recovery of 
the learner state by a later session. 
 
The experiment can also reveal limits. Function-first instruction may be less effective for some 
learners or materials; an appealing analogy may obscure formal meaning; a learner may sound 
fluent while failing an unfamiliar problem. Those outcomes would guide revisions to the teaching 
method. 
 
11. RESEARCH CONTRIBUTION AND NEXT 
STEPS 
Headmaster brings together three problems that are often treated separately: preserving an 
author’s original meaning, teaching difficult material in a usable structure, and maintaining 
evidence of an individual learner’s progress over time. 
 
The most immediate next steps are practical. Establish the installed Android baseline and 
working feature inventory. Select a primary McPhetridge text. Confirm reliable extraction and 
source pointers. Conduct the baseline assessment. Capture one full, authorized teaching 
interaction and the learner’s own post-lesson response. Evaluate the result against the original 
text. Finally, test whether a new tutor session can reconstruct the learning state accurately from 
the saved records. 
 
The longer-term study can determine whether the combination of functional mapping, exact 
learner quotations, interactive teaching, and continuity produces a measurable benefit over 
ordinary AI-assisted learning. 
 
12. CONCLUSION 
Headmaster began with a question about fidelity: how can AI help communicate a person’s 
meaning without replacing that person’s words? The DHS Translation Protocol answered by 
separating protected original language from AI-authored organization and explanation. Applied 
to education, the same principle leads to a tutor that must preserve the learner’s work while 
helping that learner build new capability. 
 
The program now exists as an actively developed Android prototype, and its educational 
premise can be tested. Mitchell D. McPhetridge’s challenging technical writing offers a 
demanding first case. If Gelhaus can learn the material through Headmaster and subsequently 
explain, question, and apply it accurately in his own words, the result will provide a concrete 
measure of the approach’s value. 
 
The central ambition is straightforward: a teacher should leave the learner able to think and 
explain independently.
