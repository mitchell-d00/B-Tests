The Object as Filter
The Object as Filter: Holographic Return, AI Continuity,
and Human-Readable Interfaces
Benjamin Gelhaus
Independent Researcher
Abstract
This paper argues that a single structural pattern recurs across three domains
usually treated as unrelated: biological perception, the foundations-of-physics debate
over observation and reality, and AI-facing continuity architecture. In each domain,
an overwhelming, undifferentiated field is made navigable not by receiving it in full,
but by filtering it into bounded, action-relevant units. Perceptual interface theory
argues that organisms do not perceive reality directly but through a fitness-tuned
interface that discards nearly all available information. A body of foundational-
physics work, most notably John Wheeler’s participatory universe proposal, has
speculated that observation itself may be constitutive of legible physical reality
rather than a passive report on a reality that already existed in full. Object-oriented
AI continuity architecture, developed across a companion sequence of papers, argues
that AI runtimes require bounded, authority-marked, relationally-situated objects
rather than raw undifferentiated context in order to act safely and recoverably. This
paper treats these three as structurally related instances of one pattern rather than
as a single unified theory, and is explicit throughout about which claims are
established, which are contested, and which remain the author’s own prior
framework requiring further definition before public presentation.
Keywords: interface theory of perception, attention and filtering, participatory
universe, AI continuity, object-oriented cognition, philosophy of perception,
foundations of physics, human-readable interfaces
This is the fifth paper in a sequence on AI-facing project structure. Artificial
Tangibility (Gelhaus, 2026a) and its companion paper (Gelhaus, 2026b) propose that
bounded objects give AI runtimes operational grip on overwhelming context.
Continuity, Authority, Directive (Gelhaus, 2026c) proposes a control grammar for
governing those objects, and Canonical Tokens (Gelhaus, 2026d) proposes a minimal-
representation principle for a specific class of them. This paper steps back from AI-
facing architecture specifically to ask a broader question those four papers assume
but do not examine directly: is the filtering pattern they describe for AI runtimes a
special case of something more general, observed independently in other domains
that have nothing to do with artificial intelligence? This paper’s claim is bounded
rather than triumphant: the pattern recurs; the recurrence is worth naming; the
recurrence does not, by itself, prove any single grand unifying theory of
consciousness, reality, or mind.
1. One Pattern, Three Domains
The pattern under examination has a simple shape. An undifferentiated field exists,
containing far more information than any single act of engagement with it can use.
Something happens to that field - evolutionary tuning, an act of measurement, a
design choice - that converts an unusable totality into a bounded, navigable, action-
relevant structure. The structure that results is not the field itself. It is a rendering of
the field, shaped by what the receiving system can use.
This paper examines three instances of that shape: biological perception, a contested
but serious position within physics regarding observation and reality, and AI-facing
project continuity. The claim of this paper is not that these three instances share a
hidden underlying mechanism in any strong metaphysical sense. The claim is
narrower and more defensible: they share a recognizable structural pattern, and that
recurrence is worth documenting carefully, with the boundaries of each domain’s
evidence kept visible rather than blurred together.
2. Perceptual Filtering: The Interface Theory of Perception
Human perception does not present reality as it is. This is not a controversial claim
within perceptual science; it is close to a starting assumption. The interface theory of
perception goes further, arguing that perception is actively shaped by evolutionary
fitness rather than by accuracy: an organism whose perceptual system reported the
true structure of reality, rather than a simplified, fitness-relevant rendering of it,
would in general be outcompeted by an organism whose perceptual system reported
only what mattered for survival and reproduction, formalized through evolutionary
game-theoretic simulations comparing fitness-tuned versus truth-tuned perceptual
strategies (Hoffman, Singh, and Prakash, 2015; Hoffman, 2019).
The often-used analogy is a computer desktop: a blue rectangular icon is not blue,
rectangular, or located where it appears in any deep physical sense, but the icon is
not a lie either. It is a functional interface that lets a user interact correctly with
underlying complexity the user does not need to perceive directly in order to act
successfully. Perception, on this view, works the same way: it is not a window onto
reality, but an interface to it, and the interface is good exactly because it hides
almost everything that is not immediately useful.
This paper uses Hoffman’s theory the same bounded way Artificial Tangibility already
does (Gelhaus, 2026a): not as proof that reality is somehow less real than it appears,
but as a rigorous, published source for the general claim that successful engagement
with an overwhelming field often depends on filtering, not full access.
3. Attentional Filtering: Learned, Individual, and Tunable
A separate and complementary body of work addresses a narrower question: even
after perception has already reduced an overwhelming field to something
perceivable, a further bottleneck exists at the level of attention. Donald Broadbent’s
filter model of attention proposed that incoming information passes through an early
selective filter before deeper processing, based on physical characteristics of the
input rather than its meaning (Broadbent, 1958). Refinements of this model,
particularly Anne Treisman’s attenuation theory, showed that the filter is not simply
on or off for a given input channel; it can be tuned by prior relevance and learned
salience, which is why a person’s own name reliably breaks through an otherwise
fully ignored background conversation.
This second filter is important for this paper’s argument because it is individually
variable in a way perceptual interface theory, being a species-level claim, is not. Two
people with the same perceptual apparatus can attend to entirely different features
of the same field, because their attentional filter has been tuned differently by
different histories. This paper treats this as the second layer of filtering: perception
supplies a species-general interface; attention, shaped by lived experience, supplies
an individually-tuned filter on top of it.
4. Observation and Reality: A Contested Position in Physics
The most speculative domain examined in this paper is the foundations-of-physics
literature on the role of observation in constituting physical reality. This section is
written with more caution than Sections 2 and 3, because the claims here are
genuinely contested within physics itself, not merely simplified for a general
audience.
John Archibald Wheeler, a major twentieth-century physicist who worked with Niels
Bohr and coined the term black hole, proposed a position he called “It from Bit” and
a related Participatory Anthropic Principle: that physical reality at its foundation may
derive from binary distinctions registered through acts of observation, and that the
universe may be best understood not as a fixed backdrop that observers happen to
appear in, but as what Wheeler called a self-excited circuit, in which observer-
participation is in some sense necessary to render the universe’s history definite
(Wheeler, 1990). Wheeler’s own language was deliberately provocative: he proposed
replacing the word “observer” with “participator,” and described reality as arising
from “billions upon billions of acts of observer-participancy.”
This paper does not adopt Wheeler’s position as established fact, and it would be a
serious overclaim to present it that way. Wheeler’s ideas remain a minority, actively
debated interpretive position regarding the foundations of quantum mechanics, not a
consensus finding, and many physicists reject the strong reading in which
consciousness itself is required to make reality determinate. What this paper draws
from Wheeler is narrower and more defensible: a serious physicist, working from
genuine physical puzzles about measurement and observation, arrived independently
at a structural claim of the same shape examined in Sections 2 and 3 - that an
undifferentiated or indeterminate field becomes definite, legible reality only through
an act of engagement by something positioned to register it. Whether that structural
claim is true at the level of fundamental physics is not something this paper can
settle. That the same structural shape recurs, independently, in a serious physicist’s
foundational work is itself the relevant fact for this paper’s argument.
5. The Fourth Instance: Objects as Filters in AI-Facing Continuity
The companion papers in this sequence describe a fourth instance of the same
structural pattern, this time in an entirely artificial and non-biological setting. An AI
runtime, given an unstructured pile of files, chat history, and instructions, faces
exactly the problem Sections 2 through 4 describe in other domains: an
overwhelming field containing far more information than can be usefully acted on
without some prior structuring.
Artificial Tangibility’s proposed solution is filtering by another name. A bounded
object - a source, a receipt, a Blackboard, a draft - is a fitness-tuned rendering of the
underlying project state, shaped not by evolutionary pressure but by deliberate
human design, for the specific purpose of letting a non-embodied runtime act
correctly without needing the totality of the project’s raw history (Gelhaus, 2026a).
Continuity, Authority, and Directive (Gelhaus, 2026c) function as the tuning
parameters of that filter: what must survive, what governs, what is safe to act on
now. Canonical Tokens (Gelhaus, 2026d) addresses the floor of that filtering process,
arguing that some references are already at their minimal correct form and should
not be re-expanded once filtered into an established shape.
The claim of this section is precise: AI-facing object orientation is not merely inspired
by, or loosely analogous to, biological and physical filtering. It is a fourth,
deliberately engineered instance of the same general problem-solving shape -
undifferentiated field, filter, bounded unit, navigable world - applied to a domain (AI
runtime orientation) where the filter is built by explicit human design rather than by
evolution or physical law.
6. Holographic Return: A Bridge Requiring the Author’s Own
Definition
This section names a bridge concept from the author’s own prior work, and it is
handled differently than Sections 2 through 5, for an important reason: unlike
Hoffman, Broadbent, Wheeler, or the companion AI-continuity papers, this concept
does not yet have a stable, precise public definition, and this paper should not
manufacture one on its behalf.
Based on the author’s prior published work and this sequence’s own recurring
themes, Holographic Return appears to name something like the following: a
fragment, if it has been filtered correctly, can carry enough of the structure of a
larger whole to permit a return to that whole without requiring the fragment to
contain the whole’s total information. This would place Holographic Return as a claim
about what a good filter preserves, not merely what it discards - a companion
concern to Sections 2 through 5, which have focused mainly on what filtering
removes.
This paper deliberately stops short of stating this as a settled claim, because doing so
would risk misrepresenting the author’s own framework with more precision than the
author has yet given it in public form. The correct scholarly move, and the one this
paper takes, is to flag this section explicitly as requiring the author’s direct definition
and review before any public release of this paper, in the same way this sequence has
flagged sensitive or under-specified material in its companion works. This section
should be treated as a placeholder marking where the bridge belongs, not as a
completed argument.
7. What This Paper Does Not Claim
Given the domains this paper touches, its boundaries deserve explicit, repeated
statement rather than a single disclaimer buried in an introduction.
This paper does not claim that AI runtimes perceive, are conscious, or have
subjective experience in any sense comparable to biological perception. The AI-
continuity instance of the filtering pattern (Section 5) is an engineered structural
analogue, not evidence of machine sentience.
This paper does not claim that Wheeler’s participatory universe proposal is
established physics. It is a genuine, serious, contested position, presented in Section
4 with that status made explicit, not as settled fact.
This paper does not claim that the recurrence of one structural pattern across four
domains proves any single underlying metaphysical theory - not conscious realism,
not panpsychism, not a designed universe, not any specific theological position the
author may hold elsewhere. Structural recurrence is evidence that a pattern is real
and worth studying across domains. It is not proof that the domains share a literal
common cause.
This paper does not yet fully state Holographic Return as a defined public claim, for
the reasons given in Section 6.
8. Why the Recurrence Still Matters, Even Bounded This Tightly
A reasonable objection to this paper is that, once every strong claim has been
bounded away, what remains is only a loose family resemblance between four
unrelated fields, interesting as trivia but not as argument.
This paper’s response is that the recurrence is more useful than that objection
allows, for a specific, practical reason demonstrated directly by this paper’s own
companion sequence. The AI-continuity instance of this pattern (Section 5) was not
developed by importing perceptual or physical theory into AI design on purpose. It
was developed independently, through direct engineering pressure - AI runtimes kept
failing in a specific, nameable way, and object-oriented filtering was the practical fix
that worked. Only afterward did the structural resemblance to Hoffman’s perceptual
interface theory, Broadbent’s attentional filter, and Wheeler’s participatory universe
become visible.
That order of discovery matters. When a practical engineering fix, arrived at
independently under real operational pressure, turns out to share its underlying
shape with findings from perceptual science and foundational physics, the correct
conclusion is not that one domain proves another. The correct conclusion is that the
underlying problem - how a bounded system makes an overwhelming field usable -
may be a genuinely general one, worth studying as its own subject, wherever it
appears.
9. Conclusion
Four domains, examined independently, converge on the same shape: an
overwhelming field, a filtering process appropriate to that field’s specific constraints,
and a bounded, navigable rendering that results. Biological perception filters through
evolutionary fitness. Human attention filters further through learned salience. A
contested but serious position in physics proposes that observation filters an
otherwise indeterminate reality into legible history. AI-facing continuity architecture
filters an overwhelming project history into bounded, authority-marked objects a non-
embodied runtime can safely act on.
This paper has tried to hold two things at once without collapsing either into the
other: the recurrence is real and worth documenting precisely, and the recurrence
does not license a single grand claim that would outrun what any one domain’s
evidence actually supports. The strongest, most defensible version of this paper’s
argument is the narrowest one: filtering an overwhelming field into a bounded,
action-relevant rendering is not a trick unique to AI system design. It appears to be a
general solution to a general problem, and AI continuity architecture is simply the
newest place it has been rediscovered, this time by engineering necessity rather than
by evolution or theoretical physics.
The Holographic Return bridge, named but not yet fully defined in Section 6, is left
open deliberately. It marks where this paper’s argument is heading, without claiming
to have arrived.
